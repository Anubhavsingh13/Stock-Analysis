## Executive Summary
Technocraft is a well-run, cash-generative, family-controlled diversified industrial exporter — the world's #2 drum-closure maker and a genuinely cost-advantaged scaffolding/formwork manufacturer — carrying a chronic loss-making textile division and a permanently high working-capital cycle. The thesis is a durable US-scaffolding tailwind (AI/data-centre capex) plus stable drum-closure cash flows, funding disciplined buybacks; the key risks are US tariffs (25–50% on the export verticals), decelerating growth (6% in FY26 vs. 16% five-year CAGR), and a full valuation (23× P/E, near a 52-week high) that leaves almost no margin of safety. **Good business, fair-to-full price — build a watchlist, don't chase.**

## BUSINESS PRIMER

Technocraft makes and exports engineered industrial products to 80+ countries. Its economics rest on two cash engines. First, **scaffolding and formwork systems** (~48% of revenue) — steel and aluminium scaffolding, and Mach One aluminium formwork — where it is vertically integrated back into its own steel-tube production, giving a real cost edge; demand is currently being pulled by a strong US industrial/data-centre construction cycle. Second, **drum closures** — the small steel caps/plugs sealing industrial drums — where Technocraft is the world's second-largest producer (after Greif) with plants in India and China, a stable, high-margin, oligopolistic niche. Around these sit a **cotton yarn / fabric / garment** textile division (yarn profitable, garments chronically loss-making at the bottom line) and a small, fast-growing **engineering & IT services** vertical (+45% in FY26 on AI-transformation work). Revenue is overwhelmingly B2B and export-led, earned as volume × realization with steel and cotton as the swing inputs.

This is an **asset-heavy, commodity-input, export-cyclical manufacturer** — best understood as a *sum of parts*, not a single P&L. The blended 16–17% operating margin hides wide dispersion: drum closures earn 18–22%, mid-cycle scaffolding 13–15% (currently depressed to ~8% in aluminium formwork), and textiles drag. The key value drivers are utilisation, the durability of the drum-closure/scaffolding cost moat, and capital-return discipline; the key risks are the **US tariff regime** resetting export economics, the **steel/cotton input cycle**, a **structurally high working-capital lock-up** (~300-day cash-conversion cycle) that permanently caps ROCE, and the **textile albatross**. A large and growing treasury (₹468 Cr of investments) and a near-75% promoter holding also colour the whole analysis — capital is accumulating faster than it is being deployed.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds
**Secular vs. cyclical decomposition of FY26 growth (+6.3%):**
- **Secular (durable):** US industrial/data-centre scaffolding demand (AI-capex driven), stable global drum-closure consumption, formalisation of Indian formwork — a genuine multi-year baseline of **~7–9%**.
- **Cyclical / one-off:** ₹20 Cr one-time quantity discount in scaffolding (Q4 FY26, non-recurring), steel-price pass-through timing, textile volatility. Q4's 19.58% EBITDA margin is flattered by these and is **not** a run-rate.
- **Input cost cycle:** Steel (HRC) rose ~25% and was passed through with a lag; cotton cycle drives textiles separately. Neutral-to-slight-headwind.
- **Geopolitical / tariff map (structural):** US tariffs of ~**50% on scaffolding** and **~27.6% on drum closures** are the single biggest swing factor — classified **structural**, requiring pricing/geographic management, not a one-quarter fix. Widen MoS.
- **Net FX:** Large net USD/EUR export exposure — INR weakness is a tailwind, appreciation a direct headwind.

**Sector verdict: Neutral, with a genuine US-scaffolding tailwind offset by a structural tariff headwind.** Do NOT embed Q4's margin or any double-digit growth as permanent.
