## Executive Summary
Garuda is an asset-light civil-construction (building EPC) company that until FY24 earned ~95% of revenue from captive contracts awarded by its own promoter group (PKH Ventures), and is now transitioning — genuinely and rapidly — to third-party work, evidenced by a ₹4,877 Cr order book anchored by real arm's-length wins (Gorakhpur Development Authority ₹1,087 Cr; Sumer Corporation ₹1,416 Cr). The thesis-defining problem is that Garuda reports ~31% EBITDA and ~23% PAT margins — roughly **double the best listed building-EPC peers** — a level that is implausible to sustain on competitively-bid, subcontracted work and strongly suggests legacy related-party pricing that will mean-revert as the government/private book ramps. Layered on top are real governance flags (parent PKH Ventures pledging Garuda shares for its *own* working capital, a failed parent IPO, related-party real-estate subsidiaries folded into Garuda, weak cash conversion, and an auditor note on unreconciled payables). The stock looks optically cheap (~13x earnings) precisely because the market already doubts the durability of these earnings — and that doubt is well-founded.

## BUSINESS PRIMER

Garuda Construction and Engineering (incorporated 2010) is a Mumbai-based civil-construction contractor that builds residential, commercial, mixed-use, industrial and infrastructure projects on an **EPC (engineering-procurement-construction) / item-rate basis**. It makes money project-by-project: it wins a construction contract, executes it over 2–5 years, and books revenue on a percentage-of-completion basis. Critically, it runs an **asset-light model** — it owns almost no plant/equipment and deploys most machinery and labour through third-party subcontractors, keeping its own balance sheet light (fixed assets were a trivial ₹2 Cr as recently as FY24). For most of its history the business was effectively a captive: it performed sub-contract construction for its promoter group, **PKH Ventures**, which still owns ~67.6%. In FY23, ~95% of revenue came from related parties. Since its October 2024 IPO (₹264 Cr; ₹174 Cr fresh + ₹90 Cr promoter OFS), it has been converting into a professionally-run third-party contractor.

This is an **asset-light, project-based services business** — the economics are driven by order-book conversion, execution velocity, working-capital discipline and, above all, **margin realisation on competitively-won work**. The key value driver is order inflow × execution × sustainable margin; the key risks are (1) **margin normalisation** — the reported ~31% EBITDA / ~23% PAT margins are ~2x the best building-EPC peers and almost certainly reflect legacy related-party pricing rather than a structural edge; (2) **governance / promoter-group entanglement** — a financially-stretched parent (failed 2023 IPO; pledging Garuda shares in July 2026 to fund *its own* working capital) that has used Garuda as the group's listed vehicle and folded related real-estate subsidiaries (PKH Ayodhya, UP World Trade Centre, PKH Projects LLP) into it; and (3) **execution step-up risk** — Garuda's largest-ever order (₹1,087 Cr Gorakhpur govt project, 30-month schedule) is a multiple of its entire prior annual revenue. Everything in this analysis should be read through the lens of *"are these margins and this governance real and durable?"*

## Module 2 — Industry Structure & Capital Cycle

### Porter Five Forces — Building EPC
| Force | Read | Signal |

| New entrants | Very low barriers — asset-light model means *anyone* with a project-management team and subcontractor network can compete | **Negative** |

| Substitutes | Low (someone must build the building) | Positive |

**Industry verdict: Structurally Unattractive-to-Neutral.** Building EPC is a low-moat, low-barrier, buyer-favoured industry. This is exactly why sustained 2x-peer margins are the central red flag — the industry structure does not support them.

### Capital Cycle (Chancellor)
Capital is *flooding into* listed construction/EPC (multiple IPOs 2023–25, including Garuda). Rising capital + capacity chasing the housing/infra upcycle is a **late-ish capital-cycle** signal for the sector — returns for the marginal contractor tend to compress from here. Garuda's reported ROIC (>40%) is *far* above the industry historical average, which — per the capital cycle framework — is a warning that it is more likely to revert down than to persist.

**The anomaly, stated plainly:** A **23% net margin** and **31% EBITDA margin** for an *asset-light, subcontracted* civil contractor is extraordinary. Listed building-EPC peers (PSP Projects, Ahluwalia Contracts, Capacité) run **~11–16% EBITDA and ~6–9% PAT margins.** Garuda is ~2x the best of them. Asset-light subcontracting normally *lowers* margins (you pass value to subcontractors), so the model does not explain it. The most probable explanation is **legacy related-party pricing**, which will compress as competitively-bid government (Gorakhpur) and developer (Sumer) revenue dominates the P&L in FY27–FY28.

**ROIC / DuPont:**
- ROCE ~42%, ROE ~31% — spectacular, but **margin-driven, not leverage-driven** (equity multiplier only ~1.4x; near-zero debt).
- **DuPont insight:** because the entire return profile rests on the abnormal margin (not on leverage or asset turns), **any margin normalisation flows almost 1:1 into ROE.** If EBITDA margin halves toward peer levels, ROE roughly halves. This is the crux risk in one line.
- ROIC ≫ WACC today → value-creating *on current margins* — but the capital-cycle and industry-structure read both say this spread is unlikely to persist.

**OCF/PAT (5-yr):** poor (<0.6 cumulative). **Earnings quality: Medium-to-Low.**
