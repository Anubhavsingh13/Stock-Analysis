## BUSINESS PRIMER

**What it does / how it makes money.** Simplex Castings makes **engineering castings and fabricated equipment** — cast-steel and iron components, and larger fabricated/EPC items — for India's core industrial sectors: integrated steel plants (SAIL/Bhilai heritage), Indian Railways (wagon bogies and cast steel components), power (BHEL/L&T), cement, defence and, newly, shipbuilding (Mazagon Dock). Revenue is essentially project/order-based: it wins orders (often approval-gated, e.g. RDSO for railways), casts to spec in its Chhattisgarh foundries, and bills on delivery. Economics are volume × realisation with a heavy raw-material (steel scrap/pig iron/ferro-alloys) and energy cost base, and long production cycles that tie up large inventory.

**What kind of business / what to watch.** This is a **deeply cyclical, capital- and working-capital-intensive, commodity-adjacent capital-goods manufacturer** — not a compounder. Its fortunes track its customers' capex cycles (steel, railways, power), so demand and margins swing hard: it earned 12–14% OPM in FY15–18, then **collapsed to −75% OPM and a ₹29 cr loss in FY20**, lost ₹17 cr again in FY23, and has only in FY25–26 reached an 18% OPM / 24% ROCE cyclical peak. The key value drivers are order inflow (railways/defence/power) and margin discipline; the key risks are **customer/segment cyclicality, powerful buyers (Railways, PSUs) with liquidated-damages clauses, input-cost and energy volatility, and a punishing working-capital cycle (inventory ~197 days, cash-conversion ~228 days) that keeps free cash flow negative even in good years.** The single most important framing: judge today's numbers as a *cyclical high*, not a new normal.

**Segment classification:** Castings/foundry → capital goods / metallurgy → covered under the Infrastructure/EPC & metals lens ✓ (order-book, working-capital-days and EBITDA-margin KPIs apply).

**Forensic verdict: Clean on governance, but low cash-conversion quality and high cyclicality.** Not manipulation — genuine capital-goods working-capital economics. Weight the negative FCF heavily.
