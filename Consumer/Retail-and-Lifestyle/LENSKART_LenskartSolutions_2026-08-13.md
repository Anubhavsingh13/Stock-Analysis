## BUSINESS PRIMER

Lenskart sells eyewear — prescription glasses, sunglasses, contact lenses — plus eye-test services and a "Gold" membership, to retail consumers (B2C) through a tightly integrated omnichannel model: ~2,700+ physical stores, a website and an app with 3D/AI virtual try-on, backed by **in-house manufacturing** (a large automated facility in Rajasthan) and an owned supply chain. Revenue is **volume × average selling price** across two engines: India (~60% of revenue, growing ~27%) and International (~40%, largely **Owndays**, the Japan/SE-Asia chain it acquired in 2022, plus Gulf/SE-Asia Lenskart stores). The economics rest on two things: (1) **gross margin (~69%)** from vertical integration and private-label design, and (2) **store-level operating leverage** — sweating fixed store and manufacturing costs across rising volumes. It sells ~2 crore+ frames a year and has ~1 crore annual transacting customers in India alone.

This is a **capital-light-ish, brand-plus-scale consumer retail compounder** — closer to a Titan/D-Mart-style formatted retailer than to a loss-making internet startup, and genuinely profitable and cash-generative (FY26 OCF ~₹1,028 Cr). The key value drivers are store-count growth, same-store sales, ASP/mix, and margin expansion as scale builds; the key risks are **valuation** (extreme), competitive intensity (Titan Eye+, global entrants, D2C copycats), international execution (Owndays integration and overseas expansion), and the usual new-age governance features — **low promoter holding (~17.5%), heavy ESOPs, and "adjusted" earnings**. The structural feature that colours the entire analysis is that this is a high-quality franchise **priced for perfection** in a euphoric IPO market — so the whole analysis is a Mauboussin expectations problem, not a business-quality problem.

## Module 8 — Valuation: PIE First (the entire decision)

### 8A. Price-Implied Expectations — the crux
At ₹587 / ~₹1.02 lakh crore, on FY26 adjusted PAT ~₹530 Cr → **~190–230× earnings**; on EV/EBITDA ~**100×**; on sales ~**11×**. Backing out what this price requires:
- To merely *earn its cost of capital* over the next ~5 years and still trade at a (rich) **~45–50× exit P/E**, Lenskart must compound PAT at roughly **~35% for five straight years** — i.e. PAT from ₹530 Cr to **~₹2,300–2,500 Cr by ~FY31** — *and* the market must still award a premium multiple then.
- That requires **flawless execution on all levers at once**: 25–30%+ revenue growth, EBITDA margin expanding toward ~20–22%, successful international scaling, no competitive margin erosion, and manageable ESOP dilution.
- **Expectations gap: strongly OPTIMISTIC (PIE > realistic base case).** Even a *good* outcome (say 22–25% growth, 18–20% margins) likely disappoints a price set for ~35% compounding. **You must beat an already-demanding bar just to avoid losing money.**
