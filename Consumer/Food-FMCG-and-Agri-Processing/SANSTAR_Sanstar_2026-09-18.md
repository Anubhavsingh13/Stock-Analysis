## Executive Summary
Sanstar is India's #2 maize wet-miller — a cyclical, commodity-linked agro-processor that has just **doubled crushing capacity to 2,350 TPD** and secured a **9% strategic stake + JV from Ingredion** (global starch major) at ₹110/share. The business is emerging from a genuine margin trough (FY26 OPM 4.8%, a Q1FY26 operating loss) and Q1FY27 confirmed a recovery (gross margin back to 33.4%). The thesis is a **volume-ramp + mix-upgrade (derivatives) recovery story**; the problem is that at ~₹110–115 the market already prices a healthy mid-cycle recovery — trailing P/E ~50–65× on trough earnings, and even on normalized EBITDA the EV sits at ~13–14× (peers trade 8–11×). The single biggest risk is **doubling capacity into a soft-spread window**: revenue and returns depend on maize-realization spread and on ramping new capacity to 75%+ utilization. Excellent business trajectory and strategic validation, but the price offers no cushion — a watchlist name with a buy-below discipline, not a buy at CMP.

## Business Primer
**What it does / how it makes money.** Sanstar buys maize (corn), wet-mills it, and separates it into **native starch, modified starches, liquid glucose, dextrose** and co-products (**gluten, germ/oil, fibre, steep liquor**) sold as food, animal-nutrition and industrial inputs. It is almost entirely **B2B**; ~34% of sales are **exports to 34 countries** (higher realization). Revenue is *volume × realization*, where realization is anchored to maize cost plus a **processing spread**. Two plants: Kutch (Gujarat) and Dhule (Maharashtra). The economics depend on (a) the **grinding spread** and (b) **capacity utilization** (fixed-cost-heavy plant).

**What kind of business / what to watch.** An **asset-heavy, commodity-cyclical spread business** — not a branded compounder. Reported revenue is *distorted by maize pass-through* (revenue falls when maize falls even if volumes rise), so headline revenue growth misleads. Real drivers: **EBITDA/tonne and utilization**. Key risks: maize/starch **commodity cycle**, **operating leverage** (margins collapsed to 4.8% and posted a quarterly operating loss at the bottom), and the **capital-cycle risk of doubling capacity into a margin trough**. Three structural features colour everything: (1) earnings are deeply cyclical so trailing P/E on trough EPS is meaningless — value on *normalized* EBITDA/tonne on the full 2,350 TPD base; (2) **Ingredion** (Corn Products Development Inc.) took ~9% at ₹110 + a JV — strong strategic validation, and the reason promoter holding fell (dilution, not selling); (3) a **Dhule derivatives facility commissioning in FY27** is the mix-upgrade catalyst toward higher, more stable margins.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds
**Verdict:** Neutral, cyclically recovering off a trough.
**Secular vs cyclical decomposition:** The FY24→FY26 revenue fall (₹1,110cr → ₹785cr) was **predominantly cyclical/price** — maize prices normalized after the FY24 tightness, deflating realizations, while margins compressed on a soft spread. The **secular** baseline (processed food, pharma, paper, animal-nutrition demand + India-as-corn-wet-milling-export-base vs China) is a durable mid-single-digit-to-low-double-digit volume tailwind. **Do not extrapolate the Q1FY27 gross-margin snap-back (24.3%→33.4%) as permanent** — a chunk is cyclical spread recovery + co-product strength.
**Input cost cycle:** Maize softened FY25–FY26 (spread-supportive) but is a two-way risk — ethanol-blending diverts maize and can re-tighten it.
**Competitive capacity pipeline (key risk):** The sector saw **simultaneous capacity addition** — GAEL, Sanstar (2×), Sukhjit, Gulshan and new entrant **Regaal Resources** (2025 IPO) — in 2023–26. Industry-wide supply can compress spreads regardless of Sanstar's execution. This is the central sector caution.
**FX:** ~34% export revenue is a modest natural USD long; INR depreciation is a mild tailwind to realization.

**Revenue growth trajectory:** Decelerating then negative FY24→FY26 on price; **Q1FY27 +21.5% YoY** — inflecting up as volumes from new capacity + spread recovery kick in. Track *tonnes and EBITDA/tonne*, not revenue.
**Volume/Price/Mix:** Historical CAGR was **volume-led** (capacity 550→1,100 TPD) with price noise; forward growth must come from (a) **new volume** (1,100→2,350 TPD) and (b) **mix** (derivatives). Mix is the durable, higher-quality driver.

**Operating leverage:** High DOL — the swing from ~9% OPM (FY24) to 4.8% (FY26) with a quarterly operating loss demonstrates it. Utilization ramp is the single biggest earnings lever.
**FCF inflection:** Capex peak was FY25–FY26 (Dhule). FCF inflects positive **FY27–FY28** as capex drops to maintenance and new capacity generates cash — *provided* derivatives capex doesn't extend the cycle.
**Guidance accuracy:** Short public history (listed Jul-2024); capacity target was **beaten** (built 1,250 TPD vs 1,000 guided) — an early positive.
