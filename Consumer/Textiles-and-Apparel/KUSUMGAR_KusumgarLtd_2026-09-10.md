## Executive Summary
Kusumgar is a niche, high-margin maker of engineered (woven/coated/laminated) synthetic fabrics with a genuinely differentiated position in aerospace & defence fabrics — a real, hard-to-enter business earning ~25% EBITDA margins and ~19% ROCE. But three things make the current setup unattractive: (1) it is a **100% Offer-for-Sale IPO** — the company raised zero growth capital and promoters monetised ~15% of the business; (2) FY26 revenue and profit **de-grew** (₹806→₹676 cr sales; ₹109→₹88 cr PAT standalone) with **sharply deteriorating working capital** (debtor days 59→117, WC days 30→112) and weak cash conversion (CFO negative in FY25); and (3) the stock has run ~40% above its ₹419 IPO price to a **P/E of ~47–62**, richer than larger, cleaner peer Garware Technical Fibres (~36×) which itself is de-growing. The business is a lumpy, order-driven defence supplier — attractive to own at the right price, but the market is currently pricing sustained 20%+ compounding onto a company that just shrank. **Verdict: quality business, wrong price.**

## Business Primer
Kusumgar manufactures "engineered fabrics" — synthetic base cloth (polyamide/polyester) that it weaves and then coats or laminates with polyurethane and other chemistries to give it specific performance properties (fire resistance, chemical resistance, weatherproofing, tensile strength). It sells these B2B across four segments: **Aerospace & Defence fabrics and solutions** (parachutes, inflatables, protective and ballistic textiles, defence-grade covers — the crown-jewel, high-margin segment), **Industrial & Automotive** (mechanical rubber goods substrate, automotive interior fabrics), and **Outdoor & Lifestyle** (luggage, weatherproof gear, tents). Revenue is roughly volume × realisation on ~1,000 SKUs, ~75% domestic and ~25% export, produced from six vertically-integrated facilities in Gujarat (weaving through coating/lamination in-house). The economics depend on two things: defence/industrial customers qualifying and re-ordering its specialised SKUs (high switching cost once designed-in), and its ability to hold coating/lamination margins as raw-material (crude-linked polymer) costs move.

This is a **specialty-manufacturing / niche-technical-textile** business — closer to a specialty chemical converter than a commodity textile mill. The key value driver is **product mix** (defence/aerospace share of revenue) and **realisation**, not volume of plain cloth. The key risks are: lumpiness (defence orders arrive in bunches — FY26's "de-growth" was really a weak H1 followed by a very strong Q4 and Q1FY27), single-geography concentration (all plants in Gujarat), customer concentration, and a working-capital cycle that has roughly quadrupled in two years. A structural feature colouring the whole analysis: the IPO was **100% OFS** — no primary capital came in, so the listing is a liquidity event for the promoter family, not a growth-funding event, and there is no fresh-capex story attached to the offer.
