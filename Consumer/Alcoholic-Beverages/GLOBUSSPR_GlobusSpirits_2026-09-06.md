## Executive Summary
Globus Spirits is a "grain-to-glass" hybrid — roughly **half commodity ethanol/ENA distiller, half branded liquor (IMIL + premium IMFL)** — that has grown revenue from ₹586 Cr (FY15) to ₹2,710 Cr (FY26) and is now executing a sensible pivot: **capex cycle done, self-funding, cutting debt**, while trying to re-rate itself from a "cheap ethanol commodity play" into a "consumer premiumisation story." The investable thesis is that its fast-growing Prestige-&-Above (P&A) portfolio scales to a profitable, mid-teens-margin consumer business (management targets >₹2,200 Cr consumer revenue at >17% margin by FY29), which would justify a consumer multiple on a rising share of EBITDA. The key risk is that **~55% of the P&L is still policy-driven commodity ethanol at administered ₹5–7/litre margins with growth capped by the E20 blending ceiling, while the premium engine that justifies the 28× P/E is still loss-making (P&A EBITDA −₹9.4 Cr in FY26)** — so at ₹914 (28.6× P/E, ~12–13× EV/EBITDA, 2.4× book, ROE 8.8% below cost of capital) you are paying a consumer price for a mostly-commodity, unproven-premium business.

## Business Primer
**What it does:** Two engines. **(1) Manufacturing (~52–60% of revenue):** six grain-based distilleries (~334 million-litre capacity, North & East India) making **ENA** (base spirit for liquor), **ethanol** (sold to OMCs for petrol blending under the EBP programme), bulk alcohol, and **DDGS** animal-feed by-product. A commodity-spread business: grain (mainly maize) in, administered ethanol/ENA price out. **(2) Consumer (~40–48%):** own brands — **IMIL** mass "country liquor" (strong in Rajasthan/Haryana/West Bengal — Nimboo, Ghoomar) and a scaling **IMFL/P&A** premium portfolio (Terai gin, DÖAAB whisky, Governor's Reserve). Plus franchise bottling. Customers span B2G (ethanol→OMCs), B2B (ENA), and B2C (brands→state liquor channels).

**What kind of business:** A **capital-heavy, policy-driven agri-commodity processor fused to a branded, state-regulated consumer premiumisation bet** — two halves with different economics, cyclicality and deserved multiples that must be valued separately (SOTP). Manufacturing value driver = ethanol/ENA volume × grain-spread × utilisation; master risk = it's a price-taker between volatile maize and government-set ethanol prices. Consumer value driver = P&A mix/brand traction; structural constraint = state excise. Two features frame everything: the stock has **de-rated ~27% (₹1,253 → ₹914)** as the FY21 ethanol windfall normalised and premium burned cash, and the company has **exited its heavy-capex phase** (net debt/EBITDA ~1.75×, maintenance-capex only, debt-reduction focus) — so the story now is harvest-and-premiumise, not build.
