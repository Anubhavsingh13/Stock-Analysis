## Executive Summary
Cyient is a professionally-run, near-debt-free engineering R&D (ER&D) services company — aerospace, rail/mobility, communications, energy — that has hit a multi-quarter **growth wall**: its core DET (Digital, Engineering & Technology) segment is flat-to-declining in constant currency, FY26 PAT fell ~29% (₹648→₹463 Cr), and a new loss-making **semiconductor** venture is a drag (EBIT −16.7%). The thesis is a *quality-at-a-reasonable-price recovery bet*: strong balance sheet, ~46% dividend payout plus a ₹720 cr buyback, a stated path to a 15% DET EBIT margin (now pushed to H1 FY28), and improving margins even as growth stays soft. The key risk is that ER&D demand (aerospace/discretionary) stays weak, the 15% margin keeps slipping, and the semiconductor bet burns cash — leaving a fair-value stock with no near-term catalyst. At ₹1,048 (P/E ~28.6×) it is neither cheap nor expensive; the re-rating needs *topline growth to return*, which has not yet happened.

## Business Primer

Cyient sells engineering brainpower, not products. Its dominant **DET segment (~74% of revenue)** provides outsourced R&D and digital engineering to global OEMs: designing aircraft sub-systems and doing aftermarket engineering for aerospace, signalling and rolling-stock engineering for rail/transportation, network engineering for telecom, and geospatial/utility work for energy. Revenue is people-led (billed hours × rate × headcount, increasingly with fixed-price and outcome-based deals), earned largely in USD/EUR from a concentrated set of long-tenured anchor clients (B2B, export-heavy). The second engine is **DLM — Design-Led Manufacturing (~26%)** — the separately-listed electronics-manufacturing subsidiary Cyient DLM (Cyient owns ~52%), which builds high-reliability electronics for defence/aerospace/medical. A third, newer bet is a **Semiconductor** design/turnkey business that is currently loss-making and being scaled toward breakeven around late FY27/FY28. The two things the DET business depends on are (a) sustained R&D outsourcing budgets at its anchor OEMs (aerospace especially), and (b) its ability to move up the value chain into higher-margin digital/software and fixed-price work.

This is a **capital-light services compounder that has temporarily stalled**. The key value drivers are constant-currency revenue growth (currently the weak link) and EBIT-margin expansion (currently the bright spot); the key risks are cyclicality/discretionary-spend cuts at OEM clients, an aerospace supply-chain overhang (Boeing/Airbus rate issues), and capital being diverted into a sub-scale semiconductor venture. Structurally important features: the company is **founder-professionalised** (Bodanapu family, ~24% promoter holding — low, so board/institutional-governed), **near-debt-free with strong FCF**, and returns cash steadily (dividend + buyback) — i.e. governance and balance-sheet quality are high; the problem is *growth*, not solvency or integrity.
