## Executive Summary

## BUSINESS PRIMER

DMCC makes industrial and specialty chemicals built on three "chemistries." The **sulphur** platform runs from bulk sulphuric acid and oleum (it was India's first sulphuric-acid producer) up to higher-value sulphonated derivatives (benzene sulphonic acid and related intermediates used by pharma, agrochem, dyes and textiles). The **boron** platform makes boric acid, borax and specialty boron compounds. The newer **ethanol** platform extends into ethanol-based derivatives. Revenue is essentially **volume × realisation** of these chemicals sold B2B to pharma, agrochemical, textile and industrial customers — and because a large slice is still relatively bulk/commodity chemistry, realisations (and therefore reported revenue) move with input prices, above all **sulphur**. That is exactly what happened in FY26: sulphur prices rose, sulphuric-acid pricing followed, revenue jumped 35% — but margins compressed because the higher prices were pass-through, not value capture.

This is best classified as a **cyclical, semi-commodity specialty-chemical company attempting a mix-shift up the value chain** — not (yet) a capital-light branded compounder. The key value driver management is chasing is **margin/mix** (more downstream sulphur/boron/ethanol derivatives per tonne), while the key risk is the **commodity cycle** (sulphur, sulphuric-acid spreads) that still dominates the P&L, plus **capex execution** on the derivative/Dahej expansion and the **working-capital swing** that turns cash flow negative in rising-price years. The structural feature to keep front-of-mind: **reported revenue growth here can be low-quality (price pass-through) and must always be decomposed into price vs. volume vs. mix before it is believed** — FY26 is a textbook case where 35% headline growth masked margin erosion and cash burn.
