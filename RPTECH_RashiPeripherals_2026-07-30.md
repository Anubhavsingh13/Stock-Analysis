## Executive Summary
Rashi Peripherals (brand "RP tech India") is one of India's two large national distributors of IT hardware, components, peripherals and enterprise technology — a wafer-thin-margin (~2% EBITDA, ~1.8% PAT), working-capital-intensive, asset-light business that has just delivered a record FY26 (revenue ₹15,827 Cr, +14.9%; PAT ₹282 Cr, +34%). The thesis bulls are buying is a genuine tailwind stack — AI-PC refresh, a 131%-growing semiconductor distribution vertical, enterprise/data-centre mix shift and Dell/NVIDIA/new-brand additions. The problem is price and quality: the stock has risen ~170% in one year and now trades at ~18x earnings, a ~50% premium to larger, higher-ROCE peer Redington (12x), while the underlying business has a narrow-at-best moat, chronically negative free cash flow, and single-digit-percent margins where a small gross-margin slip or inventory/rate shock erases a large share of profit. **Biggest risk: margin compression + working-capital/inventory blow-up in a business with almost no margin buffer.**

## BUSINESS PRIMER

Rashi Peripherals is a **B2B technology-products distributor**: it buys hardware in bulk from global brand principals and resells it through a channel of ~10,000+ resellers, system integrators, retailers and e-tailers spread across ~700 towns via 55 city branch offices and ~50 warehouses. Revenue is essentially **volume × price of goods traded** — the company earns a thin gross spread plus vendor incentives/rebates and after-sales-service income, not a manufacturing margin. It reports two segments: **Personal Computing & Enterprise Solutions (PES ~58% of FY26 sales)** — laptops, desktops, components (CPUs, GPUs, motherboards), servers, storage, networking, and a fast-growing semiconductor/embedded distribution line; and **Lifestyle & IT Essentials (LIT ~42%)** — peripherals, mobility, gaming, lifestyle tech and consumables, which carries structurally better gross margins. It distributes 50+ global brands (historically Intel, AMD, ASUS, NVIDIA, Western Digital, Seagate, Logitech, HP peripherals; recently added Dell commercial and Teachmint). The two things the economics absolutely depend on are (1) **retaining and adding high-value brand mandates** and (2) **turning inventory and receivables fast enough** that the thin spread compounds into an acceptable return on capital.

This is a **low-margin, high-turnover, working-capital-driven trading business — asset-light in fixed assets but asset-heavy in net working capital.** The key value driver is not price or product but **capital velocity** (inventory turns and cash-conversion cycle) and **scale** (buying power, vendor rebates, fixed-cost absorption over a bigger revenue base). The key risks are **margin fragility** (a 30–40 bps gross-margin move swings PAT by double digits), **inventory obsolescence/price-erosion** in fast-moving tech SKUs, **customer/vendor concentration**, **interest-rate sensitivity** (the working-capital book is debt-funded), and **negative operating cash flow whenever it grows fast.** A structural feature colouring the entire analysis: as a distributor it is a **price-taker on both sides** — principals set product prices and terms, resellers demand credit — so it can rarely raise its own take-rate; growth in rupee profit comes mainly from scaling volume and mix, not from pricing power.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds
**Secular vs. cyclical decomposition (critical):** FY26 revenue growth +14.9%; H2 growth was **~20–22% price-increase-driven** (component cost inflation passed through), i.e. *not* volume. Decompose the demand:
- **Secular (durable):** India IT-hardware penetration, digitisation, data-centre/enterprise capex, semiconductor/embedded distribution, AI-PC adoption (25% of PCs sold now AI-enabled). Structural baseline ~**10–12%**.
- **Cyclical (fades):** Windows-11/PC-refresh bulge, component-price-inflation pass-through, GPU/AI-server spot demand. Est. **~5–8pp** of current growth is cyclical.
- **Anchor for DCF:** durable ~**12–13%** revenue CAGR, **not** the 20%+ management aspiration.
- **Input-cost / inventory cycle:** Rising component prices *help* nominal revenue but *raise* inventory-obsolescence and price-erosion risk (a stock write-down risk if prices reverse). Management itself flagged a possible **5–10% PC-shipment dip** and **H2 FY27 consumer softness** as laptop prices rise on affordability.
- **Competitive capacity:** Redington (5x larger), Ingram Micro, Supertron, Savex all chasing the same brands/verticals → structural pricing pressure; nobody has pricing power.
- **FX:** Imports USD-denominated; sales INR. Net **short USD** — INR depreciation raises COGS and WC funding; largely passed through but with a lag that can pinch margin in a sharp move.
- **Sector verdict:** **Tailwind (demand)** — but a *low-quality* tailwind: it lifts revenue, not margin. Duration of the AI-PC/semiconductor leg: multi-year structural; the price-inflation leg: transient.

**Per skill guidance for asset-light businesses, the PPE/Gross-Block AT model is replaced by the relevant capacity driver: NET WORKING CAPITAL and inventory/capital turns.** Fixed assets are trivial (₹77 Cr on ₹5,327 Cr assets); the "capacity" that generates revenue is the funded working-capital book.

### Revenue engine (working-capital-turns model)
- FY26: Revenue ₹15,827 Cr on ~₹1,200–1,400 Cr net working capital → **capital turns ~11–13x.** Revenue growth requires proportional WC funding (debt or internal accrual).
- **NWC% of sales ~8–9%** (rising with CCC). Each ₹1,000 Cr of incremental revenue needs **~₹85–90 Cr** of additional working capital.
- **Implied revenue path (base, ~13% CAGR):** FY26 ₹15,827 → FY27 ~₹17,900 → FY28 ~₹20,200 Cr. Funded by internal accrual + modest incremental debt; **ICR must stay > 3x** — flag if borrowings outrun EBITDA.
