## BUSINESS PRIMER

IG Petrochemicals makes **phthalic anhydride (PA/PAN)** — a bulk petrochemical intermediate made by oxidising **orthoxylene (OX)**, a crude-oil derivative. PA goes into plasticizers (for PVC cables, footwear, leather-cloth) and alkyd resins (paints). IGPL runs five PA plants on one integrated 275,000-MTPA campus at Taloja, Maharashtra, giving it ~50% of India's PA capacity and the position of lowest-cost domestic producer and second-largest single-site producer globally. Revenue is essentially **volume × realisation**, where realisation is set by the global PA price and profitability is set by the **PA–OX spread (the "delta")**. ~90% of output is sold domestically. By-products — maleic anhydride (IGPL is India's only producer), benzoic acid and diethyl phthalate — add modest value. The company is now building **downstream**: an Advanced Plasticizer plant (mechanically complete Mar 2026, ramp through FY27, ~₹1,000 Cr peak revenue, 10–12% gross margin) that captively consumes its own PA, plus a pyrolysis-oil unit (CY2026).

This is a **commodity / cyclical chemical business, not a specialty chemical.** The single value driver is the PA spread; the single dominant risk is that spread compressing when global PA supply outruns demand — which is exactly what happened in FY24–FY26 as ~300kt of new Asian capacity came online. There is no branded pricing power: IGPL is a price-taker whose only structural edge is cost (scale, single-site efficiency, backward integration, and freight/import-parity protection on the domestic 90%). Earnings therefore have almost no floor in a bad cycle (FY26 consolidated PAT was ₹2 Cr) and a high ceiling in a good one (FY22 PAT ₹264 Cr). Any analysis must be read through **normalised, through-cycle** earnings — never a single quarter, up or down. The downstream plasticizer push is management's attempt to dampen this cyclicality by moving up the value chain.

**Segment:** Commodity Petrochemicals / Basic Chemicals (adjacent to the covered "Specialty Chemicals" reference, but PA is a *commodity* — the relevant KPI is the PA–OX spread and utilisation, not specialty-chem order-book/export mix). KPI reference applied with that adjustment. ✓

## Module 3 — Business Quality & Moat

**Greenwald's three barriers:**
1. **Cost advantage — PARTIAL/YES.** Lowest-cost Indian producer; single-site scale, backward integration, logistics/import-parity protection on the 90% domestic book. This is real but **relative** — it improves position *within* the spread, it does not create the spread.
2. **Customer captivity — NO.** Commodity; customers switch on price.
3. **Local economies of scale — YES (bounded).** ~50% domestic share + freight-protected home market give regional dominance.

**Buffett pricing-power test: FAIL.** IGPL cannot raise prices 10% and hold volume — PA is import-benchmarked.

**Moat verdict: NARROW** — a cost-advantaged, locally-scaled commodity producer. The moat protects *relative* profitability and survival through troughs (it stayed cash-generative when spreads collapsed), but it does **not** deliver durable high ROIC. Through-cycle ROCE ~15% with 3%–45% swings.

**Fisher 15-point:** Adequate on market potential, cost control, management depth; weak on margin stability (commodity) and product differentiation. **Point 15 (Integrity): PASS** — no governance veto. Estimated ~30/45.
