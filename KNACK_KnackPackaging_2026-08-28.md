## Executive Summary
Knack Packaging is a high-ROCE, export-oriented maker of woven-polypropylene bulk bags (FIBCs / PLWPP) that has grown profit at ~35% CAGR and lifted operating margins from 13% to 18% over five years on premiumisation, operating leverage and a benign polymer cycle. The thesis is capacity-led: a ~₹435 Cr new plant (IPO-funded) roughly doubles capacity into a global bulk-packaging market growing only ~5%, so the bet is whether the new tonnes get absorbed at premium mix *without* triggering the FIBC industry's classic overcapacity-and-price-war reflex, and whether the polymer-spread windfall proves durable. The key risk is that this is a **cyclical, capital-cycle-sensitive commodity converter with a thin premiumisation edge, listed only seven weeks ago** — so there is no through-cycle listed track record, no concall/guidance history to score management on, and no valid weekly technical base yet. Business quality is genuinely good; the sensible stance is to watch two–three quarters of post-listing delivery and PP-cycle behaviour before committing.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds
**Verdict: Neutral, with a genuine structural tailwind offset by an emerging capital-cycle headwind.**

**Secular vs. cyclical decomposition (critical here):**
- **Secular (durable):** China+1 sourcing shift toward Indian bulk packaging; substitution of multiwall paper bags by durable/recyclable PLWPP; food-safety/formalisation. But the *end market itself grows only ~4.8–5.0% CAGR* (global flexible bulk packaging $86.6B→$109.3B by CY29). **Durable baseline growth: ~5–8%** (market + modest share gain + mix).
- **Cyclical (fades):** FY24→FY26 margin expansion rode a **soft, stable polypropylene price** window (good spread) plus post-COVID export normalisation. The Q1FY27 +40% revenue spurt is off a low base + fresh capacity commissioning — **not** a new run-rate. **Do not embed >8–10% as permanent, and do not embed the 18–20% OPM as guaranteed through a PP up-cycle.**

**Input-cost cycle:** PP granule = 55–65% of COGS, tracks crude/naphtha → rising PP would compress the spread; current window benign.
**Competitive capacity pipeline (the real headwind):** Knack is doubling capacity *at the same time* as listed and unlisted FIBC peers expand — the textbook setup for oversupply and price pressure. Watch industry-wide capex.
**Geopolitical/FX:** ~44% export revenue, US is the single largest export geography → **US tariff / antidumping-duty risk** (Indian FIBC exporters have faced US/EU AD investigations historically). Net FX exposure is a modest tailwind on a weak INR but a two-way risk.

**Moat verdict: NARROW.** A genuine but shallow moat resting on integration + certifications + relationships at the premium end; none of the three barriers is wide or proven across a cycle.
