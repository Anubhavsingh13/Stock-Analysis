## Executive Summary

SPARC is the innovation R&D arm demerged from Sun Pharma in 2007, controlled by the Dilip Shanghvi promoter family (65.7%). It develops novel molecules (NCEs) and novel drug-delivery systems, monetising them via out-licensing (mostly to parent Sun Pharma) rather than selling drugs itself. It has lost money nearly every year of its existence, funded by promoter capital. The **thesis is binary pipeline optionality**, not fundamentals: FY26 delivered a genuine validation event — FDA approval of **Sezaby® (neonatal seizures)**, which generated a Rare-Pediatric-Disease PRV that SPARC sold for **$195M**, plus a **₹600 Cr promoter preferential** — together giving the company, for the first time, a multi-year self-funded R&D runway. **The key risk:** the residual ~₹5,000 Cr enterprise value is being ascribed to an **early-stage, mostly Phase-2 pipeline** (vodobatinib, vibozilimod/SCD-044, SCD-153, SBO-154) with high clinical-failure probability and no near-term recurring cash flows. This is a venture-style bet dressed as a listed equity — appropriate only for investors sizing it as a small, high-risk option, not as a 2–3 year fundamental compounding position.

## BUSINESS PRIMER

**What it does / how it makes money.** SPARC (BSE: 532872 | NSE: SPARC) is a pharmaceutical *research* company — it discovers and develops new chemical entities (NCEs) and novel drug-delivery/formulation technologies, takes them through pre-clinical and clinical trials, and then earns money by **out-licensing** them to commercial pharma companies (overwhelmingly its own parent, Sun Pharma) in exchange for upfront payments, milestone payments and royalties. It does **not** run a conventional generics factory or sell branded drugs at scale. Revenue is therefore lumpy, event-driven and tiny in ordinary years (₹70–240 Cr), consisting mainly of licensing income and service fees; in extraordinary years a single event (a product approval, a milestone, or — in FY26 — the sale of a regulatory voucher) can dwarf everything else. Its economic engine depends on exactly two things: (1) **scientific success in the clinic** (a molecule clearing trials and regulatory approval), and (2) **someone — historically Sun Pharma — willing to license and commercialise it.**

**What kind of business / what to watch.** This is a **pre-profit, R&D-optionality (venture-style) company**, closest in character to a listed clinical-stage biotech, wrapped inside an Indian promoter structure. The value driver is **pipeline progression and binary trial read-outs**, not price/volume/margin; the dominant risks are **clinical failure, perennial cash burn, dilution/promoter-funding dependence, and single-customer concentration on the Sun Pharma parent** (a related-party channel). Two structural features colour the entire analysis: first, reported "earnings" are almost never operational — FY26's profit is a **one-off voucher sale**, so any multiple based on it is meaningless; second, the company has historically survived only because the **promoter keeps funding it**, which makes promoter intent and related-party terms as important as the science. Judge it on cash runway + risk-adjusted pipeline value, never on P/E or ROIC.
