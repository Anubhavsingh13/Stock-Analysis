## Executive Summary
Tempsens is India's largest manufacturer of contact and non-contact temperature sensors (thermocouples, RTDs, infrared/pyrometry) and one of the largest electrical-heater makers, plus a fast-growing specialised-cable business — a genuine niche-leader in industrial process instrumentation with real indigenisation/customisation barriers, exports to ~28% of revenue, low debt and clean books. The thesis risk is **price, not business**: at the ₹300 issue the stock is already full at ~36x FY26 earnings (no listed peer to anchor against), and at the ~₹610 GMP-implied listing it is ~73x — a price that discounts flawless execution for years, leaving nothing on the table for a 2–3 year investor. The single most important structural fact is that **₹555 cr of the ₹650 cr issue (85%) is an Offer-For-Sale by the Talesara family branch and one individual selling near-zero-cost stock — while the two core Rathi promoters (who own the majority) sell nothing.**

## BUSINESS PRIMER

**What it does / how it makes money.** Tempsens (Udaipur, Rajasthan) is a B2B thermal-engineering and cable-solutions company. It designs and manufactures three product families: **Temperature Sensing Solutions** (thermocouples, RTDs, thermowells, infrared/optical pyrometers, calibration — ~44.6% of FY26 revenue), **Electric Heating Solutions** (industrial heaters, furnaces, elements — ~34.7%), and **Specialised Cables** (thermocouple/instrumentation/compensating cables — ~20.7%). Revenue is volume × realisation on largely customised, engineered-to-order products sold to industrial OEMs and process industries (steel, cement, glass, power, chemicals, aerospace, oil & gas). ~67% of revenue is **Projects/OEM** and ~33% is **MRO** (recurring replacement) — the MRO tail gives some annuity stickiness. Operations are heavily Udaipur-based (Units I–VII) plus subsidiaries/JVs in Germany, Poland, UAE, Indonesia and Korea; exports were ~28% of FY26 revenue. The two things the business depends on: **indigenised/customised manufacturing capability** (the entry barrier) and **industrial-capex + MRO demand** across its end-markets.

**What kind of business / what to watch.** This is an **asset-heavy, niche-engineering manufacturer** — not a commodity, but not a wide-moat compounder either. The key value drivers are volume growth (capacity + new geographies) and mix (higher-value sensing/heating vs. commodity cable), and the key risks are (a) it has grown FY24→FY26 substantially by **acquisition/amalgamation** (Marathon Heater, Tempsens GmbH, Poland, UAE) rather than purely organically — this inflates the reported CAGR and clouds the "restated" 3-year numbers; (b) **working capital is stretching** (net working-capital days rose from 152 → 210 over two years); and (c) acquisition goodwill/intangibles depress reported ROE (13.5%) well below the underlying ~20%+ ROCE. The structural feature that colours the whole analysis: **there is no listed comparable in India** (per the F&S report cited in the RHP), so the market has no valuation anchor — which is exactly why an 184x-subscribed, ~100%-GMP listing can overshoot violently.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds
**Verdict: TAILWIND (structural), partly cyclical.**
- **Secular:** India manufacturing/industrial capex up-cycle, PLI-driven factory build-out, China+1 sourcing shift toward Indian instrumentation, import-substitution/indigenisation of sensors and heaters, MRO replacement demand. Durable.

## Module 2 — Industry Structure & Capital Cycle
**Porter Five Forces verdict: NEUTRAL-to-ATTRACTIVE (niche).**

- **Substitutes:** Low near-term (temperature measurement is mission-critical in process industries); some tech shift toward non-contact/IR and digital/IIoT sensing — Tempsens participates in both.
- **Rivalry:** Fragmented; global majors (WIKA, Emerson, Honeywell, ABB) at the high end, unorganised players at the low end; Tempsens is the **India leader by revenue** with no listed domestic peer.

Not yet a returns-destroying overcapacity signal, but the frothy primary market is a yellow flag.

**Industry verdict:** Structurally decent niche → but **scales up the required margin of safety** because there is no peer benchmark and the growth is partly bought, not earned.

## Module 10 — Forward View: 2–3 Year Thesis
