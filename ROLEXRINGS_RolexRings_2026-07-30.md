## Executive Summary
Rolex Rings is one of India's top-5 forging companies and a leading global maker of hot-rolled forged & machined **bearing rings** (sold to SKF, Timken, Schaeffler, NRB, NBC) and **automotive forged components**. FY26 was a "clean-up" year: revenue was roughly flat at ₹1,143 cr, the company ended the 2013 CDR legacy by settling its ~₹101 cr Right-of-Recompense (RoR) liability (₹51.6 cr exceptional charge, dropping reported PAT to ₹141 cr vs. ₹193 cr adjusted), moved to net-cash of ₹367 cr, and announced a ₹180 cr buyback. The **thesis** is a debt-free, cash-generative, cost-competitive supplier levered to a cyclical recovery in global bearing/auto demand (management guides 15–17% FY27 growth as US tariffs normalise). The **key risk** is that the business has *plateaued for four years* (revenue stuck in the ₹1,010–1,222 cr band since FY22, ROE compressed from ~31% to ~16%) while the stock at ~14.7× EV/EBITDA and ~19.5× adjusted earnings already prices in the recovery — leaving little margin of safety and only ~10% expected annualised return with poor downside asymmetry.

## BUSINESS PRIMER

**What it does / how it makes money.** Rolex Rings, based in Rajkot (Gujarat), forges steel into two product families. First, **bearing rings** (~47% of FY26 revenue, ₹539 cr) — the hot-rolled, near-net-shape forged and machined rings that go inside ball and roller bearings; these are sold B2B to the world's largest bearing makers (SKF, Timken, Schaeffler, NRB, NBC). Second, **automotive & general-engineering forged components** (~45%, ₹520 cr) — gears, shafts, and forged parts for auto and industrial OEMs. Revenue is fundamentally **volume × realisation**, where realisation is steel price plus a value-add conversion margin, so gross margin (51.5% in FY26) is largely a steel pass-through and the real economics sit at the EBITDA line (~20%). It runs ~1,65,000 MTPA of installed capacity across three Rajkot units (26 forging lines, 625 machining spindles), serves 57 customers across 14 countries, and splits revenue roughly 56% domestic / 44% exports. Seven of its top-10 customers have been with it 15+ years — qualification and re-qualification cycles at bearing OEMs are long, which is the source of whatever stickiness exists.

**What kind of business it is / what to watch.** This is a **cyclical, asset-heavy, B2B auto-and-industrial ancillary / forging supplier** — a "picks-and-shovels" vendor to the bearing industry rather than a branded product company. Its key value drivers are volume (capacity utilisation) and mix (higher-value bearing rings vs. commodity forgings); its key risks are (a) the **global auto/industrial capex cycle**, which it does not control — and, tellingly, its own largest customers (Timken, SKF, Schaeffler) effectively *set* that cycle; (b) **steel price** and FX; and (c) **customer concentration**. A structural feature that colours the whole analysis: the company deleveraged aggressively from 0.70× D/E (FY21) to net-cash today, which is excellent for risk but mechanically halved its reported ROE — the "30% ROE" of the IPO years was partly financial leverage, and a ₹367 cr idle-cash pile now drags returns. The FY26 numbers must always be read in two versions — *reported* (after the one-time RoR settlement) and *adjusted* — or the P&L looks far worse than the underlying business.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds
**Verdict: Neutral, early-cyclical recovery attempt.**

**Secular vs. cyclical decomposition (the critical step).** Management guides 15–17% FY27 growth. Decomposed: this is *overwhelmingly cyclical, not secular*. The drivers management itself cites are (i) recovery of the ~30% of US export revenue lost to tariffs (peaked >50%, now normalised to 25%) — pure cyclical/one-off reversal; (ii) European order ramp of ₹160–165 cr/yr — partly structural (China+1 share gain) but front-loaded; and (iii) domestic bearing-ring demand that grew 50% YoY in FY26 off a low base — cyclical restocking plus some share gain. The **durable structural baseline** for a forging supplier tied to global auto/industrial output is closer to **8–11%** (nominal-GDP-plus-mild-share-gain). The 15–17% FY27 print, if achieved, should NOT be extrapolated into the DCF as permanent.

**Input-cost cycle:** steel is broadly stable-to-soft; gross margin already at a cyclical-high 51.5%, so limited further tailwind — model flat-to-slightly-lower gross margin. **Inventory cycle:** the sharp FY26 domestic bearing recovery has restocking characteristics; watch whether it sustains into FY27. **Competitive capacity:** the broad Indian forging complex (Bharat Forge, Ramkrishna Forgings, MM Forgings, CIE) is NOT aggressively adding bearing-ring capacity aimed at Rolex's niche, but global bearing OEMs can and do dual-source. **Geopolitics/FX:** the live risk is **US tariffs** on exports (44% of revenue is export; a chunk US-bound) — classified as *structural-transient* (re-routable partly to Europe, but tariff level is a policy variable outside company control). Net FX exposure is a modest tailwind on INR weakness given ~44% export revenue against largely INR costs.

## Module 1c — Stock Cycle: Company-Specific Positioning
**Classification: Late-cycle plateau / early-recovery attempt — the ambiguous zone.**

- **Revenue-growth trajectory:** *Decelerated and flat-lined.* Revenue: FY22 ₹1,010 cr → FY23 ₹1,179 cr → FY24 ₹1,222 cr (peak) → FY25 ₹1,155 cr → FY26 ₹1,143 cr. Four years essentially sideways after the post-COVID surge. This is a **warning flag** even though recent quarters (Q4 FY26 +7.7% YoY) are turning up.
- **Volume/price/mix:** the FY26 story is mix (bearing rings +11%, autos −10%) rather than volume, and margin (gross margin +210 bps). Mix improvement is the most durable of the three, which is a mild positive; but total volume has not grown.
- **Physical capacity:** ~1,65,000 MTPA installed; utilisation is well below the ~78–80% peak of FY23. There is **ample headroom to grow 2–3 years on ₹30–40 cr/yr debottlenecking capex without a major greenfield** — so the cyclical recovery is physically deliverable.
- **Operating leverage:** high DOL (asset-heavy, ~20% EBITDA on a large fixed base). Utilisation recovery → meaningful margin uplift; conversely a demand slip hits EBIT hard. DFL is negligible (net cash). Net: EPS is highly sensitive to the top line via operating leverage, not financial leverage.
- **FCF inflection:** already past it — net-cash, maintenance-level capex, strong OCF. FCF is structurally positive; the question is deployment (buyback answers part of it).

## Module 3 — Business Quality & Moat
**Moat Verdict: Narrow.**

**Greenwald's three barriers:**
1. **Supply/cost advantage — PARTIAL YES.** Scale (top-5 Indian forger, 1,65,000 MTPA), backward-integrated ring-rolling, Rajkot labour-cost cluster, and 20% EBITDA vs. ~15% for forging peers (Ramkrishna) evidence a genuine cost edge. But a well-funded entrant *could* replicate it with capital + time.
2. **Customer captivity — PARTIAL YES (the real moat).** Multi-year OEM qualification, tooling co-development, and 15+ year relationships with 7 of top-10 customers create switching friction. But captivity runs *both ways* — the customers are far larger and can dual-source; Rolex cannot raise price 10% and keep volume (fails Buffett's price test).
3. **Local economies of scale — NO** (global-supplier model, not a bounded local monopoly).

**Durability tests:** ROIC/ROCE has stayed > WACC across the cycle (FY23 peak ~32% ROCE → FY26 ~20% ROCE, still above ~13% WACC) — a real, if shrinking, spread. Market-share is small but rising in the global bearing-ring pool. **Pricing power: FAIL** (steel pass-through; no independent pricing).

## Module 6 — Earnings Quality, ROIC & DuPont

**ROIC/ROCE (reported):** FY23 ~31.8% → FY24 ~24.4% → FY25 ~21.0% → **FY26 ~20.1%.** WACC ~13%. **Spread positive but narrowing** — a cyclical margin/utilisation give-back plus a growing idle-cash drag, not (yet) moat erosion. Ex-cash operating ROCE is materially higher (~28–30%), which is why the business still earns its keep.
