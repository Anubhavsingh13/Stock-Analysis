## Executive Summary
Medicamen Biotech is a research-led generic-formulations and oncology/sterile-injectables maker, controlled (~40%) and consolidated by Shivalik Rasayan. It carries the **identical financial signature as its parent — only worse**: ROCE has fallen from 16% to ~5%, operating margins from 21% to 10%, and **operating cash flow is not just negative but deteriorating sharply (−₹6 → −₹7 → −₹8 → −₹33 Cr over FY23–FY26)** while working capital has swollen to a 259-day cash-conversion cycle. In FY26 it plugged the cash hole with a large equity raise (reserves jumped ₹200→₹262 Cr). The bull case is real but distant — **USFDA-approved oncology and sterile-injectable facilities** that could open regulated (US/EU) markets. But at ~33× earnings and ~1.3× book on 4% ROE, the price pays for that success in advance while the cash statements scream caution. **Buying Medicamen directly gives you a purer pharma-formulations play than Shivalik, but with even weaker cash conversion and the same "growth that doesn't turn into cash" problem.**

## Business Primer

Medicamen Biotech is a Haridwar-based pharmaceutical company that develops, manufactures and markets **generic finished-dosage formulations** (tablets, capsules) and, increasingly, **oncology formulations and sterile injectables**. Revenue is B2B/institutional — it supplies branded generics and tender/export orders, including to Africa, LatAm and other semi-regulated markets, with a strategic push into **regulated markets (US/EU)** on the back of USFDA approvals for its oncology and injectable facilities. Economics are volume × realization on generic molecules, where the mix is slowly shifting from low-margin plain generics toward higher-value oncology and injectables. The company is **controlled by Shivalik Rasayan (promoter, 40.46%)**, which took management control in 2016; the remaining ~59% is public float, so a direct investor here sits as a minority alongside Shivalik.

This is a **capital-heavy, transitioning generic-pharma business** whose value driver is the same as the parent's: **margin and asset-turn recovery as regulated-market/oncology volumes ramp**. The risk stack is nearly identical — collapsing returns on capital, negative and worsening operating cash flow, extreme working-capital intensity (receivables 151 days, inventory 251 days), rising debt *and* fresh equity dilution, and dependence on winning slow, uncertain regulated-market qualifications. The structural feature that colours everything: Medicamen and Shivalik are **financially conjoined** — Medicamen's negative OCF is a large part of why the *consolidated* Shivalik entity also bleeds cash. Analysing one without the other is incomplete.
