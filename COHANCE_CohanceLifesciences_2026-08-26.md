## Executive Summary
Cohance Lifesciences is an Advent-owned, newly-merged (Suven Pharma + Cohance) Indian CDMO/CRAMS platform spanning small-molecule APIs, antibody-drug-conjugate (ADC) payloads, oligonucleotides and specialty chemicals, now run by former Cipla CEO Umang Vohra. The thesis is a *cyclical trough with structural optionality*: management has explicitly called Q1 FY27 (Jun-2026) "the lowest quarter ever" (EBITDA margin 2.2%, a net loss), with recovery weighted to H2 FY27 and acceleration into FY28+ on ADC/oligo ramp and a large restocking order. The key risks are that the stock trades at ~120–160× trailing earnings on genuinely depressed numbers, a multi-year Advent PE-exit creates a persistent supply overhang, promoter pledge is 94.6%, and the technical structure is below a declining 200-DMA — i.e. a good business at a price that already discounts a clean recovery that has not yet begun.

## Business Primer

Cohance Lifesciences is a contract development and manufacturing organisation (CDMO) — it does not sell its own branded drugs; it makes molecules and chemistry for other pharmaceutical and specialty-chemical companies under long-term contracts. Revenue flows from three engines: (1) **API+ (~48–60% of sales)** — complex active pharmaceutical ingredients, notably CNS/psychiatric APIs where the company claims >50% global market share in certain products; this is the high-certainty "bedrock"; (2) **Pharma CDMO (~39%)** — custom development and manufacturing of small molecules, plus the newer, higher-value **ADC payloads** (via US subsidiary NJ Bio) and **oligonucleotides/nucleic acids** (via Seppala); and (3) **Specialty Chemicals** — contract manufacturing for agrochem/industrial customers. Customers are global innovator and biotech companies (B2B, export-heavy), and revenue is inherently lumpy because it depends on customer shipment schedules, destocking/restocking cycles and biotech funding. The two things the business absolutely depends on are (a) deep, multi-year "anchor" customer relationships and differentiated chemistry (ADC, oligo, niche CNS), and (b) regulatory-clean, audited manufacturing sites.

This is a **capital-light-ish, IP/chemistry-led compounder in the making, currently behaving like a cyclical** — the key value drivers are volume (capacity ramp of new ADC/oligo lines) and mix (shift to niche high-margin technologies), and the key risks are customer/order concentration and lumpiness, US-FDA regulatory action (a Form 483 was issued at Pasha Milan; Nacharam remediation is ongoing), and — decisively for this name — an unusual ownership structure. The company is controlled by private-equity house **Advent International**, which assembled it via two acquisitions and a merger (effective 1 May 2025), pledged 94.6% of promoter holding as acquisition financing, and has begun **selling down** (an 8.9% block for ₹3,094 cr in Sep-2025). That PE-exit overhang, plus post-merger accounting that makes every year-on-year comparison pro-forma and noisy, colours the entire analysis: reported multiples are distorted, earnings are at a trough, and the float is being actively expanded by the controlling seller.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds
**Secular vs. cyclical decomposition — critical here.** The Indian CDMO sector rides a genuine **secular China+1 / supply-chain-diversification tailwind** and a structural shift of innovator/biotech outsourcing to India (ADC, oligonucleotides, complex APIs are multi-year growth verticals). But Cohance's *reported* collapse is almost entirely **cyclical/transient**: pharma-CDMO destocking and customer shipment phasing (CDMO revenue −38.7% YoY in Q1 FY27), biotech-funding-driven delays in NJ Bio FTE renewals, and two molecules losing patent protection (a one-time reset already in the base). Durable structural baseline: high-single/low-double-digit for API+, with ADC/oligo as the incremental growth layer.
- **Input/FX:** Export-heavy → INR depreciation is a tailwind to gross margin (management cited it); raw-material sourcing partly China-linked (structural risk, re-routable over time).
- **Competitive capacity:** Indian CDMO peers (Divi's, Piramal Pharma, Syngene, Neuland, Concord Biotech) are all adding ADC/complex capacity — watch for oversupply in payloads over 2–3 yrs.
- **Sector cycle verdict: Tailwind (structural) overlaid by a transient destocking Headwind.** Primary driver: customer destocking + biotech funding — expected to ease over FY27, with H2 recovery guided. Do **not** embed the current trough as the run-rate, and do **not** embed China+1 as unlimited permanent growth.
