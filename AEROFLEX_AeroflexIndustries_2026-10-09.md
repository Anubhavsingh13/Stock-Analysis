## Executive Summary

Aeroflex makes stainless-steel flexible hoses, hose assemblies and metal bellows for 80+ countries. In the last year it has built a second engine: **SFN liquid-cooling skid assemblies for AI data centres**. Q1 FY27 was its best quarter ever: **revenue ₹146 Cr (+72% YoY), EBITDA ₹33.5 Cr (23.0%), PAT ₹18.8 Cr (+162%)**. Skids alone contributed **₹32.4 Cr (23% of revenue; 1,040 skids at ~₹3.1 lakh)**, up from ₹18.9 Cr in Q4 FY26. Skid capacity was raised from 6,000 to 9,000/yr on 1 Jul 2026 and is going to **15,000/yr by Oct–Nov 2026** for only ₹48 Cr of capex. Hose capacity rises from 17.5 to 20 mn m by Q3. Management reaffirmed **30–35% FY27 revenue growth** and a **25% EBITDA margin** target ([Q1 FY27 call](https://www.bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/AttachHis/6401402c-a5ca-4785-87db-477dd4e8d73c.pdf)).

It is a genuinely good business: ROCE 19–36% since FY22, debt-free, cash-backed earnings, 20%+ revenue growth. **The price has run far ahead of it.** The stock tripled in a year (₹159 in Jan 2026 → ₹524; 52-week high ₹584). It trades at **103× TTM EPS, 15.5× book (94th–96th percentile of its post-IPO range) and ~60× FY28E base EPS**. The base-case DCF is ₹106–172, and even the bull DCF tops out at ₹269. Our 2-year scenarios (bear ₹160 / base ₹390 / bull ₹650) give an expected value of **₹398 (−24%)** and **U/D 0.35:1**, so the hard stop applies. The data-centre story is real but early, and it runs through a single Indian channel partner today. A buyer at ₹524 pays now for a skid business that would have to reach several times its 15,000-unit capacity.

## Business Primer

Aeroflex designs and manufactures environment-friendly metallic flow products. The core is corrugated stainless-steel flexible hoses (17.5 → 20 mn metres/yr), sold bare or as engineered assemblies with braiding and fittings. These serve HVAC, oil and gas, industrial process, solar, semiconductor, railways, firefighting and, increasingly, data-centre applications. It also makes metal bellows (including miniature bellows) and, through its Hyd-Air subsidiary, precision components. Since FY26 it has assembled **SFN (secondary fluid network) liquid-cooling skids**: the piping-and-manifold modules that circulate coolant across a data-centre floor, sold to an Indian partner who serves the data-centre builder. Historically ~80% of revenue was exported (Europe, USA, 80+ countries; [Outlook Business, IPO](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/aeroflex-industries-gets-sebi-s-clearance-to-float-ipo-news-307439)). Domestic share has risen with skids. Returns depend on capacity utilisation, the assembly/skid mix (bare hose ~16–20% EBITDA, assemblies ~22–26%, per the Q1 call), and the pace of data-centre construction.

This is a **niche, export-led precision manufacturer (capital-moderate compounder) with a new, high-growth, customer-concentrated data-centre vertical**. The key value drivers are volume (capacity fill), mix (assemblies and skids) and the number of liquid-cooled megawatts commissioned (~40 skids per MW). The key risks are concentration in the skid channel, design-approval-driven lumpiness, entry of global players (Parker, Senior) and stainless-steel/freight costs. Structural features: Aeroflex is a 65.5% subsidiary of the listed Sat Industries group (now Aeroflex Enterprises), listed only since Aug 2023, so long-run market data is short. Its valuation has moved from "industrial hose maker" to "AI-infrastructure play" within six months.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds

**Secular (core hoses):** Replacement of rubber with metal hoses (fire safety, durability), HVAC, process industry, semiconductor and solar build-outs, and China+1 sourcing by Western OEMs. Durable growth is ~12–15% (management sees the hose segment at 15–20%; peak hose revenue at full 20 mn m capacity of ₹650–675 Cr).
**Secular (data centres):** India data-centre capacity of ~1.6 GW is set to grow toward ~9–10 GW by 2030–31 (Morgan Stanley via the STL Q1 FY27 call), and AI racks increasingly need liquid cooling. At ~40 skids/MW × ~₹3.1 lakh, **1 GW of liquid-cooled load ≈ 40,000 skids ≈ ₹1,250 Cr of skid content**. Even if only a third of India's ~8 GW of additions is liquid-cooled, that is ~₹3,000–3,500 Cr of skids over five years, against Aeroflex's 15,000-unit capacity (~₹465 Cr/yr at full).
**Cyclical:** Data-centre construction is lumpy and depends on financing and power availability. Q1 FY27 also carried West Asia freight inflation (call).
**Competition:** "Among the few" Indian skid makers today, but Parker and Senior have entered skids globally, and local players are starting up. Skid capacity costs little, so the barrier is customer qualification (10–11 months for a new partner), not capital.
**FX:** Export-heavy core (historically ~80% of revenue) → long USD/EUR; INR weakness helps.

**Sector cycle verdict: STRONG TAILWIND (multi-year, secular), early-stage in India data centres.** Durable baseline for the DCF: ~15% core + skid ramp to the 15,000-unit ceiling, then growth tied to new capacity.

## Module 2 — Industry Structure & Capital Cycle

**Five Forces:** entry barriers **moderate** (hose know-how, certifications and global distribution are real barriers in the core; skids are qualification-led but cheap to build), supplier power **moderate** (SS coil and nickel-linked; freight), buyer power **moderate in the core** (fragmented global OEMs and distributors) and **high in skids** (one channel partner; data-centre builders specify designs), substitutes **low** (rubber → metal is the substitution trend in Aeroflex's favour), rivalry **moderate** (global: Parker, Senior/Flexonics, Witzenmann; domestic: few). **Verdict: Attractive (core) / Neutral (skids).**

**Capital cycle:** Aeroflex's capex/depreciation was 3.5–6.9× in FY24–26, and it is now finishing. Data-centre liquid cooling globally is in the "capital flooding in" phase (integrators, CDU makers and skid makers all expanding), but skid capacity is cheap and flexible: management says the machines can be repurposed for bellows and industrial piping. **Signal: early-to-mid capital cycle for India data-centre cooling; returns high, competition arriving.**

## Module 9 — Valuation: PIE First, Then DCF

### PIE
TTM NOPAT (EBIT ₹90 Cr × 0.75) ≈ ₹67.5 Cr → EV/NOPAT 102× → implied perpetual g = (6,871 × 12% − 67.5)/(6,871 + 67.5) = **10.9%**, essentially WACC.
