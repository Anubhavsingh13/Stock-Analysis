## Executive Summary
Paushak is India's largest specialty phosgene-derivatives producer — a genuinely high-barrier, oligopolistic niche within the Alembic group. The thesis is a classic capex-cycle setup: the company has spent ~₹200cr+ (debt-funded) on a new multi-purpose plant now commissioned, so reported returns (ROCE 8%, ROE 7%) are at a decade trough while revenue has been flat at ~₹210–219cr for three years. The problem is price: the stock trades at ~38–45x depressed earnings and ~3x book, which already embeds a successful multi-year ramp — while the technical picture (down ~22% in a month, ~25% in three months) says the cycle is turning down, not up. Quality is real; the price and timing are not.

## BUSINESS PRIMER

**What the business does and how it makes money.** Paushak makes specialty and custom phosgene derivatives — chloroformates, isocyanates, carbamoyl chlorides, carbamates and carbonates — at its Panelav (Gujarat) complex, which houses a 14,400 MT/annum phosgene plant, the largest such capacity in India. Phosgene (COCl₂) is an extremely toxic gas that is difficult and dangerous to transport, so downstream users prefer to buy the finished derivative from a permitted, safety-compliant producer rather than handle phosgene themselves. Revenue is B2B and flows as volume × realization across a portfolio of derivative molecules sold into agrochemicals, pharmaceuticals, dyes, plastics/polymers and perfumery, split between domestic and export customers; the company also runs a custom-synthesis / CDMO line for tailored intermediates. The economics depend on two things: (1) the regulatory/safety moat around phosgene handling, and (2) demand from the agrochemical and pharma intermediate value chains, which is itself cyclical.

**What kind of business this is and what to watch.** This is a capital-light-turned-capital-heavy niche specialty chemical franchise — historically a high-ROCE, cash-generative compounder (ROCE 25–32% in FY15–19) that has just pushed through a large capacity-expansion cycle and is now in the ramp/digestion phase. The key value drivers are volume (filling the new capacity) and mix (higher-value custom molecules); the key risks are (a) the ramp taking longer than the market expects, (b) agrochemical-cycle weakness and Chinese pricing pressure in commodity-ish derivatives, and (c) a large treasury/investment book (~₹150cr of Alembic-group and market investments) whose "other income" now supplies a distorting share of profit — other income was ~51% of Q4FY26 profit before tax. A structural feature to keep front of mind: this is a promoter-controlled (67.3%), tightly-held, thinly-traded small-cap where the founder family's other listed entities (Alembic Pharma, Alembic Ltd) sit in the same group — governance and related-party lenses matter more than usual.

## MODULE 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds

**Secular vs. cyclical decomposition.** Paushak's *reported* growth is currently near zero (3-yr sales CAGR ~1%), so there is no growth narrative to deflate — the opposite problem. The durable secular baseline for Indian specialty phosgene chemistry is ~8–12% (agrochem + pharma intermediates + import substitution + custom synthesis). The FY23 revenue jump to ₹212cr (from ₹150cr) was largely a one-off price/mix and post-COVID restocking spike that then mean-reverted — a caution against extrapolating any single strong year. The forward driver is **capacity-led** (new plant volume), not end-market acceleration.

- **Input cost cycle:** Chlorine, CO, toluene/aromatics-linked feedstocks — broadly stable-to-soft in 2025–26; not a margin headwind currently.
- **Inventory cycle:** The agrochemical channel went through a deep 2023–24 destocking that suppressed intermediate demand across Indian specialty chem; a gradual restocking is underway but uneven. Paushak's own inventory days ballooned to ~292 (FY26) vs ~180 (FY24) — partly ramp-related stocking, partly slow offtake. Watch closely.
- **Competitive capacity pipeline:** Multiple Indian specialty chem players commissioned capacity in the same FY24–26 window → sector-wide oversupply risk in commodity-ish derivatives; Paushak's phosgene niche is more protected than most, but the new multi-purpose block competes in less-protected custom/CDMO space.
- **Geopolitical/China risk:** Chinese oversupply and aggressive pricing in agrochemical intermediates is the single biggest sector headwind of 2024–26. Phosgene handling barriers insulate Paushak more than commodity peers, but not fully.
- **Net FX exposure:** Partial exporter; net USD exposure modest and not the swing factor.

**Sector cycle verdict: NEUTRAL-to-HEADWIND.** Primary driver: agrochemical destocking/Chinese pricing pressure, expected to be a multi-quarter-to-~2-year overhang, easing but not resolved. **Downstream effect:** do not embed a sharp demand snap-back; require a 5–10pp wider MoS on the sector headwind.

## MODULE 1c — Stock Cycle: Company-Specific Positioning

**Revenue growth trajectory (quarterly YoY):** Decelerating-to-negative. Q3FY26 revenue was ‑0.9% YoY; 9MFY26 PAT ‑32.6% YoY. The rate is **decelerating** — a warning even though the balance sheet is sound.

**Revenue decomposition:** Historical CAGR was mix + price led (specialty molecules), with volume capped by the old plant. Forward growth must come from **volume** on the new capacity — the most capacity-bounded and ramp-dependent of the three drivers.

**Physical capacity model:**
- Phosgene: 14,400 MTPA (the gating input for all derivatives).
- New **multi-purpose plant (~₹175cr)** commissioned FY26 — commercial production started, phased rollout over ~a year. This is a greenfield/brownfield block for new molecules and custom synthesis; standard ramp = 25–35% utilisation Yr1 → 60–70% Yr1-end/Yr2 → 85–90% Yr2–3.
- Gross block signal: net fixed assets jumped from ₹152cr (FY24) to ₹377cr (FY26); CWIP cycled ₹190cr (FY25) → ₹26cr (FY26), i.e., ~₹160cr+ transferred to productive PPE. Capacity is in the ground; the question is fill rate.
- **Capacity ceiling year:** Not a near-term concern — the constraint is under-utilisation, not a ceiling.

**Operating leverage:** High DOL in a fixed-cost chemical plant. With utilisation depressed, a modest volume recovery drives outsized EBIT recovery (and vice-versa). This cuts both ways and is why the stock is a leveraged bet on the ramp.

**FCF inflection:** FY25 and FY26 were deeply FCF-negative (‑₹122cr, ‑₹34cr) as capex peaked. FCF inflection expected **FY28–FY29**, when growth capex drops to maintenance and the new plant's working capital stabilises.

**Stock cycle classification: Early-cycle / Ramp-up (capex-heavy, margins/returns suppressed, revenue not yet accelerating).** MoS adjustment: standard-to-wider; position small until the ramp is visible in numbers.
