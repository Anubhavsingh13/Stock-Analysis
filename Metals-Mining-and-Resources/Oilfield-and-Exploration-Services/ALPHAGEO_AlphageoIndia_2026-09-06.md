## Executive Summary
Alphageo is India's only listed pure-play onshore **seismic services** company — a deeply cyclical, asset-heavy oilfield-services microcap whose revenue is a derived function of ONGC/Oil India's exploration budgets. Its FY17–19 super-cycle (₹69 Cr → ₹429 Cr) was almost entirely the one-time **National Seismic Programme**; when NSP completed in Jan 2020 and COVID crushed oil, revenue fell off a cliff and the company has been loss-making or breakeven for most of the seven years since (consolidated net loss ~₹14 Cr in FY26). The thesis is a **cyclical turnaround** (India's push to cut crude-import dependence is reviving E&P tendering, and Alphageo won ~₹250 Cr of ONGC/Oil India orders in 2024–25), backstopped by a **net-cash, below-book balance sheet**; the key risk is that demand is **binary and single-buyer** — a dry tender year empties the book — and, having already rallied ~2× off its ₹165 low to ₹324, the stock is now pricing in the recovery rather than offering it cheaply.

## Business Primer
**What it does:** Founded 1987 (CMD Dinesh Alla), Alphageo shoots and processes 2D/3D seismic surveys — sending vibrator-truck crews and recording gear into the field so oil & gas explorers can image sub-surface rock before drilling. Revenue is **project/tender-based and order-book driven**: win a fixed-value contract, mobilise crews, recognise revenue as survey km/sq-km are executed. Customers are essentially **ONGC + Oil India (both government)**, with occasional mineral/infrastructure/groundwater work and a small offshore subsidiary (Alphageo Offshore Services). Economics hinge on (1) the size/timing of India's exploration budget and (2) crew utilisation — idle crews between contracts burn fixed costs.

**What kind of business:** An **asset-heavy, deeply cyclical oilfield-services microcap** with extreme customer concentration and total dependence on a government capex cycle — the textbook "trough/recovery" cyclical. Key value driver = **volume (survey activity) × utilisation**; key risk = **binary demand** (two customers, one end-market) plus PSU **collection/claims risk**. Structural features colouring the whole analysis: zero debt, a large treasury/net-cash position, trades **below book (₹324 vs ₹352)**, illiquid (₹204 Cr m-cap, ~15k holders), and a long-term **energy-transition overhang** on hydrocarbon exploration.

## ⭐ SPECIAL FOCUS — Why Did Revenue Decline, Is It Permanent, and What Are the Triggers?

### Why revenue collapsed (the NSP boom-bust)
| FY | Consol. Sales (₹Cr) | What was happening |
|---|---|---|
| FY15–16 | 73 / 90 | Normal pre-NSP baseline (small onshore surveys) |
| **FY17** | **306** | **NSP awarded** — DGH/MoPNG's ~₹2,800 Cr National Seismic Programme (launched 2016) to acquire 2D data across all Indian sedimentary basins; Alphageo won large multi-basin packages |
| **FY18** | **429** | NSP peak execution — multiple crews deployed simultaneously |
| **FY19** | **405** | NSP tail |
| FY20 | 268 | **NSP substantially completed** (last big Ganga-Punjab/ONGC package finished first week of Jan 2020); **no new orders** in Dec-2019 quarter; COVID + oil-price crash hit E&P from Mar-2020 |
| FY21–23 | 143 / 155 / 66 | Trough — sparse tendering; FY23 the low point (₹66 Cr) |
| FY24–26 | 99 / 126 / 112 | Partial, choppy recovery on new ONGC/Oil India wins — but crews re-mobilising, so **still loss-making** |

**The decline was not company failure — it was the completion of a one-time national programme with no successor of the same scale, compounded by the 2020 oil crash.** Revenue peaked because NSP was an extraordinary, finite, government-funded data-acquisition mega-project; it fell because that project ended and India's routine exploration budget is a fraction of NSP.

### Is the decline permanent?
**No — it is cyclical/programme-driven, not structural erosion of the franchise — but with two important caveats:**
- **Cyclical, reversible:** Alphageo retains the crews, equipment, PSU pre-qualifications and market leadership that let it win work whenever tendering revives. It has already demonstrated this — winning ₹118.63 Cr (ONGC) + ₹131.63 Cr (Oil India) + ~₹35 Cr (offshore subsidiary) in 2024–25. So the earning power is intact when orders exist.
- **Caveat 1 — no NSP-scale successor is confirmed.** The recovery depends on year-to-year PSU budgets, not a committed multi-year pipeline. Visibility is lower-quality than an EPC/defence backlog. Revenue can stay volatile and sub-peak for years.
- **Caveat 2 — secular energy-transition overhang.** Onshore hydrocarbon *exploration* is a structurally challenged 10–20-year end-market. The 2–3-year cyclical case can be real even while the very-long-term case is questionable. **Do not model the 2024–25 order surge as permanent growth.**

**Verdict: a durable cyclical, not a permanent decline — but also not a secular compounder. Peak (₹429 Cr) is unlikely to repeat without a new NSP; a "normal good year" is more like ₹150–200 Cr.**

### Triggers for improvement (watch these)
1. **A new national seismic programme / DGH data-acquisition push** — the single biggest upside (would replay FY17-19). No confirmed programme yet — highest-impact, lowest-visibility trigger.
2. **ONGC/Oil India exploration capex ramp** under India's import-dependence-reduction policy (target: cut ~85% crude imports) — OALP/HELP acreage expansion, "no-go" area reductions opening new basins.
3. **Sustained book-to-bill > 1×** — order inflow replenishing faster than execution; the leading indicator, visible in BSE order-win filings 2–4 quarters before revenue.
4. **Crew utilisation recovery → return to positive EBITDA** — the proof the recovery is profitable, not just top-line.
5. **Diversification wins** — mineral exploration (NMET/GSI), CBM, groundwater, or offshore (via subsidiary) reducing single-buyer dependence.
