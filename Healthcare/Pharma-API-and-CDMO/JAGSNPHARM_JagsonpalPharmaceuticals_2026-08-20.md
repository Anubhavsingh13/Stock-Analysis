## Executive Summary
Jagsonpal is a small, debt-free, domestic branded-formulations company (gynaecology, orthopaedics, paediatrics, derma) that private-equity firm Convergent Finance took control of in 2022 at ₹235/share and has been professionalising ever since — cutting working capital to 11 days, lifting MR productivity, sharpening the brand portfolio, and returning cash aggressively (200% dividend + ₹40 Cr buyback in FY26). The business is high-quality on paper — ROCE ~23%, ROE ~17%, ~21% EBITDA margin, nearly zero debt, a leadership position in progesterone/gynaecology reaching ~90% of Indian gynaecologists — but revenue has only compounded ~7–9%, and the stock trades at **32× earnings and 5.6× book**, a valuation that already prices in a growth *acceleration* the numbers have not yet shown. Two things temper the story: FY25's ₹55 Cr profit was flattered by a one-time item (the clean FY26 figure is ~₹43 Cr), and a **February 2025 USFDA warning letter + import alert on its API plant** (including initially refusing inspectors entry) is a real governance/quality blemish, even though US exposure is financially small. **This is a good business at a demanding price — you are paying up-front for a turnaround that must now deliver mid-teens growth to justify the multiple.**

## BUSINESS PRIMER

Jagsonpal makes and sells branded pharmaceutical formulations to the Indian domestic market through a prescription-driven model: ~900+ medical representatives call on doctors, who prescribe Jagsonpal's brands, which patients buy at pharmacies. Revenue is essentially (number of prescriptions × price per pack) across therapy areas where it is genuinely strong — it is a leader in the progesterone/gynaecology segment (reaching ~35,000 of India's ~39,000 gynaecologists, ranked ~#7 in gynae CVM) and holds meaningful positions in orthopaedics and paediatrics, with 17 brands in the top-5 of their molecule category. This is a classic **branded-generics / branded-formulations** business: the moat, such as it is, comes from doctor familiarity, brand equity in specific molecules, and MR relationships rather than patents. The company is almost entirely **domestic** — its tiny US/API-export footprint is where the FDA issue sits, but it is not the profit engine.

This is a **capital-light, cash-generative branded-consumer-healthcare business** whose key value drivers are volume growth (new patients/prescriptions), gentle price/mix premiumisation, and MR productivity — and whose key risks are (a) that domestic branded pharma is a **slow-growth, competitive market** where Jagsonpal has historically grown only high-single-digits, (b) governance/quality execution (the USFDA warning letter), and (c) the **valuation itself** — a PE-owned turnaround carries the expectation of a step-change in growth and margins, and the stock is priced as if that step-change is a near-certainty. The structural feature colouring the whole analysis: this is a **Convergent Finance-controlled professionalisation play** — the entire investment case rests on whether a capable PE owner can convert a sleepy legacy brand-house into a mid-teens compounder before the premium multiple de-rates.
