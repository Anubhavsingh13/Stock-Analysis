## Executive Summary
Thirumalai Chemicals is India's #2 phthalic anhydride (PAN) producer — a **commodity chemical whose profitability is a pure spread bet (PAN minus orthoxylene)** — that is simultaneously in the **deepest earnings trough of its history** (six consecutive loss quarters; FY26 consolidated net loss of ₹168 Cr) *and* mid-way through a **debt-funded expansion that has more than doubled its asset base** (consolidated CWIP ₹2,518 Cr, borrowings ₹2,175 Cr). The thesis is a classic high-risk contrarian call: if the global PAN cycle turns and the new Dahej + US plants commission on budget, operating leverage on a doubled base is powerful. The risk is symmetric and severe: they are adding commodity capacity into a globally oversupplied market, funded by leverage, with negative EBITDA covering the interest — a combination that has already forced three equity raises at prices (₹277–₹296) now well above the market (₹185). **The right posture is to watch, not own: wait for evidence of (a) PAN-OX spread inflection and (b) on-schedule commissioning before committing capital.**

## BUSINESS PRIMER

Thirumalai Chemicals makes **phthalic anhydride (PAN)** and its downstream derivatives (phthalate esters, plasticiser inputs) plus a family of **food/specialty acids** — maleic anhydride, malic acid, fumaric acid and tartaric acid. PAN is the economic engine, and it is a **commodity**: the company buys orthoxylene (OX) — a crude-oil derivative sourced largely from Reliance Industries under an assured-offtake model — and converts it to PAN. Because OX is ~80% of production cost, the entire profitability of the business is the **PAN–OX spread**, an externally-set, globally-traded margin the company cannot control. Customers are industrial B2B buyers in plasticisers, paints & pigments, construction, auto, food, personal care and pharma. Revenue is essentially **volume × realisation**, where realisation tracks a regional commodity price. Operations span Ranipet (Tamil Nadu, the flagship PAN complex), a maleic-anhydride plant in Malaysia (Optimistic Organic Sdn Bhd), and two large greenfields under construction — PAN/fumaric at **Dahej, Gujarat** (via subsidiary TCL Intermediates) and a US plant (**TCL Specialties LLC** via TCL Global BV, Netherlands).

This is a **cyclical commodity manufacturer, not a specialty-chemical compounder** — the label "specialty chemicals" flatters it. The key value driver is the **spread**, not volume or brand; the key risk is the **PAN-OX / global-supply cycle** compounded, right now, by **balance-sheet leverage taken on at the trough**. Two structural features colour the entire analysis. First, there is a **large gap between standalone and consolidated accounts**: the parent carries ₹1,512 Cr of "investments" (chiefly capital pushed down into subsidiaries to fund the greenfields), so the *consolidated* statements — where CWIP is ₹2,518 Cr, debt is ₹2,175 Cr, and the FY26 loss is ₹168 Cr (vs ₹65 Cr standalone) — are the only ones that tell the truth about risk. Second, the company is **betting the balance sheet on an expansion in a duopoly commodity during a global oversupply** — the outcome is binary and cycle-dependent, and must be analysed as such.

## MODULE 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds

**Secular vs. cyclical decomposition.** PAN demand tracks construction, autos and paints — broadly GDP-linked, so the durable secular baseline is only **~5–7% volume growth**. There is **no structural secular tailwind** in PAN itself; the recent revenue collapse is almost entirely **cyclical spread compression**, not a demand story. Any recovery in reported profit will be cyclical spread mean-reversion — it must **not** be extrapolated as permanent.

**Input cost cycle.** Orthoxylene (crude derivative) is volatile and is ~80% of cost. The margin is the spread, and the spread is presently **crushed**: peer/market data show PAN spot values fell ~9.6% in 2025, Asian operating rates 50–75%, and PAN-OX spreads compressed to roughly **$50–100/T — among the lowest in years**. This is a classic commodity trough.

**Competitive capacity pipeline — the critical point.** The Indian market is adding capacity (including Thirumalai's own Dahej 180,000 TPA line) **into an already-oversupplied global market** where China runs at low utilisation. Anti-dumping protection on Korea/Taiwan imports was recommended for discontinuation (per ICRA), raising import competition. **Multiple simultaneous capacity additions + Chinese overhang = structural pricing pressure regardless of demand.**

**Geopolitical / trade risk.** Import competition (China/Korea) and loss of ADD protection are the live risks; US plant adds tariff/logistics complexity.

**Sector cycle verdict: HEADWIND (deep cyclical trough).** Primary driver: PAN-OX spread compression from global oversupply. Duration: uncertain — early signs of bottoming (peer IGPL's Q4 FY26 EBITDA margin recovered to ~13%), but no confirmed multi-quarter inflection yet.

**Downstream effect:** Do **not** embed any spread recovery as permanent in DCF. Sector headwind → **widen margin of safety by 5–10 pp**.

## MODULE 1c — Stock Cycle: Company-Specific Positioning

**Revenue growth trajectory (consolidated, quarterly YoY): decelerating hard, then contracting.** FY26 revenue ₹1,736 Cr (-15% YoY) after ₹2,050 Cr (FY25) and ₹2,083 Cr (FY24). Standalone quarterly sales fell every quarter through FY26 (₹446 → ₹357 → ₹300 → ₹257 Cr). **Decelerating/contracting — deep warning.**

**Revenue decomposition:** The collapse is **price/spread-led, not volume-led** — realisations fell with the commodity, and margins went negative. Price is "the most fragile driver," and here it has been fully surrendered.

**Physical capacity model:** Existing PAN ~145,000 TPA (Ranipet) + Malaysia MAN. Under construction: **Dahej 180,000 TPA PAN + fumaric** and the **US TCL Specialties** plant. Consolidated CWIP of **₹2,518 Cr** (vs net fixed assets ₹1,251 Cr) means the productive asset base is being **more than doubled**, but this CWIP currently earns **zero revenue** — depressing asset turnover today and creating a large latent revenue capacity for later.

**Operating & financial leverage — the danger zone.** DOL is high (commodity, fixed-cost heavy). More importantly **DFL is now extreme**: consolidated debt ₹2,175 Cr against **negative EBITDA** ⇒ interest coverage is **deeply negative**. Interest is currently being funded from the balance sheet / fresh capital, not operations.

**FCF inflection year:** Not visible within the 2–3 year window on current spreads. FCF turns positive only when (a) capex program completes *and* (b) spreads recover enough to service the enlarged debt. Base case: **FY28 at the earliest**, entirely cycle-dependent.

**Stock cycle classification: TROUGH / RECOVERY — but a *leveraged* trough.** The reversible cause (spread compression) is identifiable, but the balance sheet removes the usual margin of safety a trough offers. → **Widest MoS; small initial size only after a confirmed cycle turn.**
