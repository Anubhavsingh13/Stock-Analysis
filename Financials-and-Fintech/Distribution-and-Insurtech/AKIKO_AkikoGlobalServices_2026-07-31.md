## Executive Summary
Akiko is a ₹382 Cr NSE-Emerge SME-listed DSA (Direct Selling Agent) that sells credit cards and personal loans on behalf of banks/NBFCs via tele-calling, feet-on-street and a digital "MoneyMantra" platform. But the business is a **thin-moat commission agency** exposed to bank payout rates and RBI regulation, and — critically — it has generated **negative operating cash flow every single year** despite rising accounting profit. That reported-profit-vs-cash divergence is the defining earnings-quality flag, and it is not cheap at ~23× on an illiquid SME line.

## Business Primer
Akiko Global Services (operating since 2018, Delhi-based) is a channel partner / DSA for major banks and NBFCs. It acquires retail customers for financial products — primarily credit cards and personal/unsecured loans — through outbound tele-calling, on-ground sales teams, corporate tie-ups and digital lead-generation, and earns a **commission/payout** from the lender for each sourced and booked account. It has been layering in AI/automation on its MoneyMantra platform to lower documentation cost. Revenue is essentially volume of cards/loans sourced × per-unit payout, so economics track both how many accounts it books and what rate banks are willing to pay distributors.

This is an **asset-light, thin-moat services/commission** business. The value driver is sourcing volume × payout rate; the key risks are (1) total dependence on bank/NBFC payout economics, which lenders cut at will, (2) customer/lender concentration, (3) regulatory risk — RBI has been tightening on unsecured retail lending and on DSA/recovery/mis-selling conduct, any of which can compress volumes or payouts overnight, and (4) SME-platform illiquidity. The structural feature colouring the whole analysis: **profits are not converting to cash** — a DSA that books revenue on sourcing but funds a growing receivable/working-capital gap can show rising PAT while burning cash, and that is exactly the pattern here.

**Segment: Financial Products Distribution / DSA (loan & card sourcing)** — not in the standard KPI reference. ## Module 4 — Financial Forensics — the core issue
- **Operating cash flow: FY24 −₹6 Cr, FY25 −₹11 Cr, FY26 −₹2 Cr — negative every year** while reported PAT was positive and rising (₹4→₹6→₹11 Cr). Cumulative OCF is materially negative against cumulative reported profit. This is the single most important fact in the analysis: **earnings are not backed by cash.**
- Financing inflows (IPO proceeds FY25, debt FY26) have been plugging the operating cash gap.
- For a commission business, persistent negative OCF points to a swelling receivable on commission income and/or aggressive revenue recognition timing relative to collection — an O'Glove-style flag (NI up, OCF down for 2+ years).
- SME-line thin float → low liquidity, higher impact cost and volatility.
**Forensic verdict:** Significant earnings-quality concern. Not proven fraudulent, but the profit-to-cash divergence is a hard caution and, under the framework, warrants a deep dive before any capital commitment.
