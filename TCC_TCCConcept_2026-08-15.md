## BUSINESS PRIMER

TCC Concept Limited is a Pune-headquartered commercial-real-estate and managed-workspace company that, since a 2022 change of control, has been rapidly rebuilt into an asset-heavy office-space developer/operator and — more recently — a data-centre infrastructure play. In plain terms it acquires or long-leases commercial buildings, fits them out, and monetises them as managed/flexible office space (a "space-as-a-service" model billed on seats/sq-ft), while also building owned real-estate assets. Revenue therefore flows from lease/licence fees on office space plus real-estate/fit-out income, and is fundamentally a function of **square feet under management × occupancy × realisation per seat**. The company is controlled by promoter **Umesh Kumar Sahay**, the same entrepreneur behind listed managed-office peer **EFC Limited** and the (in)famous carbon-credit company **EKI Energy Services**. Over FY24–FY26 it bolted on a data-centre business (a 4 MW Tier-III facility commissioned in Pune in 2025, a 7 MW facility under development in Hinjewadi, and an announced 60 MW → 100 MW campus ambition), acquired **98.78% of NES Data Pvt Ltd** via share issuance, and took a stake in e-commerce platform **Pepperfry** — again paid for in shares.

This is an **asset-heavy, capital-cycle, early-ramp real-estate/infrastructure business wearing the costume of a high-growth compounder**. The key value drivers are square footage commissioned, occupancy ramp, and realisation; the key risks are (1) **capital allocation and dilution** — the entire empire has been assembled by issuing paper (reserves ballooned from ₹170 cr to ₹1,595 cr in two years, largely share premium, while promoter holding fell from ~69% to 45.7%), (2) **an unproven, value-destructive return profile** — despite 40–70% "operating margins," ROE/ROCE sit at ~5–6%, far below cost of capital, because ₹1,700 cr of fixed assets currently generate only ~₹180 cr of revenue (asset turnover ~0.10×), and (3) **promoter track record** — the controlling shareholder's flagship EKI Energy was a spectacular boom-bust (up ~100× then down ~90%+). The single structural feature that colours the entire analysis: **book value and reported profit are inflated by paper-funded acquisitions and a fast-growing gross block whose earning power has not yet been demonstrated.** A "0.75× price-to-book" headline is the value trap to see through, not the opportunity.
