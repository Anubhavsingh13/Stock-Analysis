## Executive Summary
Arvind SmartSpaces is the Lalbhai-group (Arvind) residential real-estate developer running a largely asset-light, JV/development-management model across Ahmedabad, Bengaluru, and now Mumbai. FY26 was a "tale of two statements": **record pre-sales of ₹1,550 Cr (+22%) and record collections of ₹1,100 Cr (+17%) with ₹417 Cr operating cash flow — while the reported P&L went backwards** (PAT −13% to ₹103 Cr, revenue and EBITDA down) because Ind AS 115 recognises profit only on possession, and FY26's completed projects were smaller/lower-margin than FY25's. This recognition lag is normal for the sector, but it means the headline **44× P/E and 5.3× book, with ROE of just ~8%, badly misrepresent the business** — you must value it on pre-sales, embedded pipeline (26.9 msf ongoing + 43.5 msf planned) and net debt (~₹79 Cr, near-nil), not earnings. The bull case is real momentum + a strong parent + a big business-development pipeline (₹3,140 Cr added in FY26, guiding ₹3,500–4,000 Cr); the caution is that the stock trades near 52-week highs at a rich pre-sales/NAV multiple **just as both the Chairman and the CEO change hands** — you're paying a premium for execution continuity precisely when the people accountable for it are new.

## BUSINESS PRIMER

Arvind SmartSpaces develops and sells residential real estate — plotted developments, villas and apartments — primarily in Ahmedabad and Bengaluru, with a new premium redevelopment entry into Mumbai (Santacruz). It makes money by acquiring land (increasingly via **capital-light joint-development and development-management arrangements** rather than outright purchase), building, and selling units to homebuyers; the cash economics are driven by **pre-sales (bookings)** and **collections**, which arrive through the construction period, whereas the *accounting* profit is recognised only when a project is completed and handed over (Ind AS 115). This is why the two numbers diverge so sharply: FY26 bookings hit a record ₹1,550 Cr and cash flow was strong, yet reported revenue and PAT fell because the projects that *completed* in FY26 were smaller and lower-margin than the prior year's. The business absolutely depends on two things: a steady pipeline of well-located land tied up at attractive economics (business development), and the sales velocity + pricing to convert that pipeline into bookings and collections.

This is an **asset-light-leaning, cash-flow-and-pipeline real-estate developer** whose true value drivers are pre-sales growth, collection efficiency, margin on the embedded pipeline, and net-debt discipline — not the lumpy reported P&L. The key risks are the classic real-estate ones: **cyclicality** (residential demand is interest-rate and sentiment sensitive), **execution/approvals/timing** (profit recognition and cash both hinge on delivery), **land-cost and geographic-expansion risk** (Mumbai and larger Bengaluru bets are new terrain), and **capital deployment** (a heavy business-development spree consumes cash and can lift gross debt even when net debt looks low). A defining structural feature to weigh throughout: **the reported earnings and standard P/E are close to meaningless here** — the entire analysis must run on the pre-sales/NAV lens the sector actually uses, and it is happening against a **simultaneous change of both Chairman (Kulin Lalbhai, Nov 2025) and MD & CEO (Priyansh Kapoor, succeeding Kamal Singal).**
