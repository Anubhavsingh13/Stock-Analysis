## Executive Summary
AksharChem (India) Ltd (AIL) is a 30-year-old Gujarat dyes-intermediate & pigment maker — India's largest exporter of Vinyl Sulphone (~25% of national VS exports) and a leader in CPC Green pigment — now diversifying into precipitated silica (for tyres). The stock trades **below book (P/B ~0.86x at ₹279; book value ₹326)**, reflecting a **deep margin trough**: operating margin has collapsed from a 31% super-cycle peak (FY17) to ~4.6%, and FY26 was a **net loss (₹0.44 Cr)** despite record ₹372 Cr revenue. The **thesis is cyclical mean-reversion + silica ramp**: if dye-intermediate realisations recover and the new silica plant reaches break-even, normalised earnings power (EPS ₹20–25 in a mid-cycle year) implies fair value around/above the current price, with tangible book as a downside cushion. **The key risks are real and current:** no moat (commodity chemistry), no backward integration, a fresh **50% US tariff on Indian exports** crushing pricing, Chinese competition, a silica plant **not yet at break-even**, and a credit rating on **CARE A-; Negative** (outlook cut from Stable in Feb-2026) with margin/leverage triggers already breached. The stock has already **doubled off its ₹141 low to ₹279** on Q4FY26 recovery hopes — the easy money in the trough is gone; this is now a "buy-the-dip-with-discipline" cyclical, not a table-pounding value buy.

## BUSINESS PRIMER
**What it does / how it makes money.** AIL manufactures **dye intermediates and pigments** — chiefly **Vinyl Sulphone (VS)** and **H-acid** (raw materials that reactive-dye makers use to colour cotton textiles), **CPC Green pigment** (Copper Phthalocyanine green, used in printing inks, plastics and paints), and, since 2021, **precipitated silica** (a rubber-reinforcing filler used mainly in tyres). Revenue is essentially **volume × realisation of commodity chemicals**: it sells B2B to dye houses, ink/paint/plastic makers and (for silica) tyre companies, roughly half by export (46% FY25, ~55% in 9MFY26) to 20+ countries, half domestic. It earns money on the **spread between crude-oil-derived raw materials and product prices** — a spread that is thin, volatile, and set by global supply/demand, not by AIL. Its economics depend on two things: **capacity utilisation** (absorbing fixed costs) and the **dye-intermediate price cycle**.

**What kind of business / what to watch.** This is a **commodity specialty-chemical cyclical**, not a branded compounder — closer to a price-taker than a price-maker. The value driver is **margin (realisation vs input cost) and the cycle**, and the dominant risks are **raw-material/FX volatility, Chinese oversupply, and the absence of backward integration** (AIL buys crude derivatives on the open market, so a cost spike compresses margin immediately). Two structural features colour the whole analysis: first, **earnings are extremely cycle-sensitive** — FY17 delivered EPS ₹72 at a 31% margin; FY24 and FY26 were losses — so no single-year multiple means anything; you must value it on **mid-cycle normalised earnings and asset value**. Second, it sits inside a **promoter group** (Paru M. Jaykrishna also promotes listed peer **Asahi Songwon Colors**, from which AIL's CPC Green division was merged in 2014) — a related-party structure to keep in view. Judge it on the cycle, the balance sheet's ability to survive the trough, and whether the silica diversification can create a steadier earnings leg.
