## Business Primer

Steel Strips Wheels Ltd (SSWL) makes automotive wheel rims — it is India's largest wheel manufacturer, selling steel wheels, aluminium alloy wheels and, more recently, aluminium steering knuckles. Revenue is essentially **volume × realization**: it stamps/forges wheels and ships them to vehicle OEMs across passenger vehicles, two- and three-wheelers, commercial vehicles, tractors and off-highway equipment, plus a growing export book to Europe, Latin America and Asia. It is almost entirely a B2B business — its customers are Maruti, Hyundai, Mahindra, Tata, and global OEMs/aftermarket — so its fortunes track (a) domestic auto production volumes and (b) its ability to move up the value curve from cheap steel wheels (~₹1,000/wheel) to premium alloy wheels (~₹3,000/wheel) and machined aluminium knuckles. The two things it absolutely depends on are: steel/aluminium input availability with cost pass-through, and OEM production schedules that keep its plants (Chandigarh, Jamshedpur, Chennai, Bhuj/Mehsana) running near full.

This is a **cyclical, asset-heavy auto-ancillary** — a commodity-adjacent manufacturer with a domestic scale moat but limited pricing power against powerful OEM buyers. The key value drivers are **volume (capacity utilisation) and mix (alloy/aluminium premiumisation)**; margin is structurally thin (~10–11% EBITDA) and gets squeezed when raw-material moves outrun the pass-through lag. The dominant risks are the **auto demand cycle, input-cost/FX swings, and moderate financial leverage** (₹828 Cr borrowings). Two structural features colour the whole analysis: first, FY22–FY23 profits were **cyclically inflated by steel-price pass-through windfalls** and should not be extrapolated as normal earnings power; second, the entire thesis now hinges on **premiumisation** — alloy wheels are already 35–37% of revenue and rising, and this mix shift (not raw volume) is what can lift returns above the pedestrian ~11–12% post-tax ROIC the legacy steel business earns.

## Executive Summary
Steel Strips Wheels is India's #1 wheel maker (39% PV steel-wheel share, 53% M&HCV, 43% tractor) executing a clean, capacity-constrained growth story — plants at 95%+ utilisation, a ₹420 Cr growth-capex program adding alloy and aluminium-knuckle capacity, zero promoter pledge and an IND AA-/Stable rating. The thesis: mix-shift to alloy wheels and aluminium knuckles lifts blended margins and incremental ROIC (~17%, above WACC) even as the legacy steel business earns only its cost of capital. The key risk: it is a low-margin, OEM-dependent cyclical, and after a ~23% rally in July 2026 the stock (₹298) trades close to fair value, leaving an inadequate margin of safety for a fresh entry.

## Module 3 — Business Quality & Moat

### Greenwald's Three Barriers
1. **Supply/cost advantage:** Partial — largest scale in Indian wheels gives cost leadership, but a well-funded entrant *could* match steel-wheel economics. Alloy/knuckle process know-how is stickier.
2. **Customer captivity:** Moderate — OEM validation + co-located JIT supply create switching costs (e.g., 74% alloy share at Hyundai, 64% at Mahindra), but OEMs deliberately dual-source. **Buffett price test: FAIL** — cannot raise prices 10% against OEMs without losing volume.
3. **Local economies of scale:** **YES** — dominant domestic share (39% PV / 53% M&HCV / 43% tractor steel wheels) in a bounded, freight-sensitive product where imports are uneconomic. This is the real moat.

**Moat verdict: Narrow.** Source = local economies of scale + partial cost advantage. Durable enough to hold share, not wide enough to command pricing power.

- ROIC ~11–12% post-tax vs WACC ~12.5% → **roughly at cost of capital** on the blended base; **incremental** alloy/knuckle capex earns above WACC (see Module 10). Moat is "scale/share" not "excess-returns."

- Gross margin held ~10–13% through steel-price cycles → adequate pass-through, no pricing power beyond that.
