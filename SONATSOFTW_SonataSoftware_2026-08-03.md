## Executive Summary
Sonata Software is a mid-cap IT services company whose economically meaningful business — International IT Services (IITS), ~$328m revenue — is a Microsoft-ecosystem / data-and-AI modernization specialist earning ~19% segment EBITDA margins and ~28% ROE, currently trading at an undemanding ~20x earnings after a ~45% drawdown from its 52-week high. **The thesis tension is stark: this is a high-return, net-cash, dividend-paying business whose core engine actually shrank in FY26 (IITS revenue −2.1% in USD), sitting in a fresh CEO transition, a technical downtrend, and a mid-cap IT sector battered by weak discretionary demand, US tariff overhang and AI-driven commoditisation of legacy services.** The key risk is that cheap stays cheap — a value trap until IITS revenue growth visibly inflects positive; the opportunity is a re-rating if the new CEO reaccelerates the core toward its long-stated (and badly missed) growth ambitions.

## BUSINESS PRIMER

Sonata Software sells two very different things under one listed entity, and conflating them is the single most common analytical error with this stock. The first and economically valuable business is **International IT Services (IITS)** — software modernization, data engineering, cloud (heavily Microsoft Azure and Dynamics 365), and increasingly AI/GenAI services sold B2B to enterprises in the US, Europe and Australia. Revenue here is people-and-project based (time-and-material plus fixed-price deals), FY26 IITS revenue was ~US$328 million (~₹2,900 crore) at ~19% segment EBITDA margins, and this is where the ~28% ROE and ~31% ROCE genuinely originate. The second business is **Domestic Products & Services (DPS)** — essentially a low-margin India reselling/distribution operation for third-party software (Microsoft and other OEM licences), which reported ₹7,772 crore of FY26 revenue but only ₹289 crore of gross contribution (≈3.7% gross margin). Because DPS is pass-through in nature, Sonata's headline consolidated revenue of ₹10,701 crore and its "7% operating margin" on Screener massively overstate scale and understate profitability; the real operating economics live in IITS, and Sonata itself reports margins on a *net revenue* basis (FY26 EBITDA margin 18.4%).

This is a **capital-light services compounder with a low-margin distribution appendage** — asset-light, cash-generative, net-cash on the balance sheet, and historically an acquisition-led grower (Quant Systems, Encore, Sopris and others bolted onto the Microsoft/ISV franchise). The key value driver is IITS constant-currency revenue growth and its EBITDA margin; the key risks are (1) that its differentiation is narrow — mid-cap IT with heavy dependence on the Microsoft partner ecosystem and a handful of large clients, exposed to AI commoditising traditional application/modernization work; (2) client and vertical concentration (BFSI/TMT/retail discretionary spend); and (3) an inorganic-growth model funded partly by debt and earn-outs. The structural feature that colours the entire analysis: **always analyse IITS separately** — value it on net revenue and segment margins, treat DPS as a low-multiple distribution business, and never take the blended consolidated numbers at face value.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds
**Secular vs. cyclical decomposition:** The durable, structural demand — cloud migration, data platform modernization, and now GenAI adoption — is intact and multi-year. But the *observed* trend for mid-cap IT in FY25–FY26 has been a **cyclical downturn**: enterprise clients froze discretionary projects amid US rate/banking stress and tariff uncertainty. Sonata's IITS −2.1% USD FY26 is almost entirely this cyclical air-pocket rather than structural share loss, though AI cannibalisation of legacy application-maintenance work is a genuine *structural* overhang layered on top.

**Net FX exposure:** IITS is a near-100% net USD/foreign-currency exporter with ~19% segment margins, so INR depreciation is a tailwind and INR appreciation a meaningful headwind — but the bigger near-term FX/policy risk is the **US tariff/immigration-cost regime**, which raises delivery cost and dampens US client budgets.

**Sector cycle verdict: Headwind (cyclical), with a structural AI question mark.** Primary driver: US discretionary-demand freeze + tariff overhang; expected to be a multi-quarter, not multi-year, phenomenon *if* US macro stabilises. **Downstream effect:** do NOT embed any near-term recovery bounce as permanent DCF growth; require a wider (30%+) margin of safety given a mid-cap in a sector headwind.

## Module 3 — Business Quality & Moat

### Greenwald's Three Barriers
1. **Supply/cost advantage:** No. Sonata has no structural cost edge over larger peers.
2. **Customer captivity:** **Partial / narrow.** Genuine switching costs exist once embedded in a client's Dynamics 365 / data platform, and the deep Microsoft partnership is a real (if shared) advantage. But this is a captivity Sonata *rents* from Microsoft rather than owns.
3. **Local economies of scale:** No — it is sub-scale globally.

**Moat verdict: Narrow.** The moat is the Microsoft-ecosystem specialisation + client switching costs, not a durable structural barrier. Buffett price-raise test: **Fail** — cannot raise prices 10% without losing work in a competitive, AI-pressured mid-cap market.

- **ROIC > WACC:** Comfortably yes (ROCE 31% vs WACC ~12.5%) — a real quality signal, but the **3-year trend is declining** (51%→44%→39%→36%→29%→31%), i.e., moat *returns* are eroding (partly goodwill from acquisitions inflating capital employed).
- **Market-share stability:** Sonata is a small share of a huge TAM; IITS revenue declining while the digital-services TAM grows = **losing relative ground**.
