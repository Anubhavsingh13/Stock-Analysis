## Executive Summary
Natural Capsules is India's #2 gelatin capsule-shell maker — a genuinely profitable, export-oriented core business — that has taken on heavy debt to build a greenfield steroidal-**API** plant (Natural Biogenex, Tumkur) which is currently deeply loss-making and has pushed the *consolidated* entity into losses (FY26 net loss ₹25 cr), with **near-zero cash and an active fundraising need**. The entire investment thesis rests on the API ramp turning profitable — into a market where Chinese oversupply is crushing realisations — while the company is simultaneously under liquidity and dilution pressure. The core is real; the transformation is unproven and financed on a stretched balance sheet.

## BUSINESS PRIMER

**What it does / how it makes money.** Natural Capsules (Bangalore, incorporated 1993) manufactures **empty capsule shells** — both hard **gelatin** capsules and **HPMC** (vegetarian) capsules — which it sells B2B to pharmaceutical and nutraceutical companies in India and ~28 export countries (exports ≈ 29% of revenue). Revenue is essentially volume × realisation of billions of capsule shells; installed capacity is now ~20.25 billion capsules/annum. This capsule business is the historical, profitable heart of the company. Since ~FY22 the company has been building a **second, unrelated business — active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), specifically steroids** — through subsidiary Natural Biogenex at a new greenfield Tumkur facility (built to WHO-GMP / USFDA / EU-GMP, zero-liquid-discharge), funded largely by term debt. The API business is pre-scale, loss-making, and is the swing factor for the whole equity.

**What kind of business / what to watch.** This is a **small-cap (₹223 cr mcap) asset-heavy manufacturer mid-transformation** — a stable, cash-generative commodity-ish core (capsules) bolted to a capital-hungry, cyclically ill-timed new venture (steroid APIs). The core capsule business is a low-to-mid-teens-margin, working-capital-heavy commodity with modest pricing power and a currency tailwind; the API business is a classic "capex-into-oversupply" bet dependent on backward integration, regulatory approvals, and PLI incentives. The single most important structural feature: **standalone (capsules) is profitable but consolidated (capsules + API + interest) is loss-making** — so the reported headline loss overstates the health of the core, while the balance-sheet risk (₹162 cr debt, ~zero cash, fundraise pending) understates how much must go right for equity holders to win.

## Module 2 — Industry Structure & Moat

**Porter (capsule shells):** Moderate industry. Barriers = capital + regulatory (GMP) + customer qualification, but it is a **near-commodity** with global competition and buyer power from large pharma. Natural Capsules is #2 in India (Associated Capsules/ACG is the dominant #1) — a strong but second position.

**Porter (steroid API):** Structurally **unattractive right now** — global overcapacity (China), price-taking, high capital intensity, regulatory-gated. Attractive only if the company achieves a genuine backward-integrated cost advantage.

**Moat (Greenwald):**
- Supply/cost advantage: *Aspirational* in API (backward integration, one fermentation step moved to enzymatic route to cut cost/improve yield) but **not yet demonstrated**; in capsules, no durable cost moat vs the #1 player.
- Customer captivity: Low-moderate — capsule buyers can qualify alternate suppliers.
- Local scale: #2 in India is real but not dominant.
- **Verdict: Narrow-to-None.** The bull case is that API backward integration *creates* a cost moat over 2–3 years — currently an option, not a fact.

## ADDENDUM B — Steroid-API Peer Cross-Check (the decisive lens)

Natural's API bet must be judged against what the steroid-API business actually pays *even for the people who win it*. The cleanest benchmark just listed: **Symbiotec Pharmalab** (IPO Aug 2026), the **global corticosteroid/steroidal-hormone API leader**.

| | **Symbiotec (global #1)** | **Natural Capsules (API)** |
|---|---|---|
| Global corticosteroid share | **38.2%** | negligible (new entrant) |
| Global steroidal-hormone share | **23.8%** | negligible |
| API revenue (FY26) | **₹869 cr** | ~near-zero → ₹70 cr *guided* FY27 |
| EBITDA margin | **26.6%** | negative (segment loss ~₹8 cr in Q1) |
| PAT margin | 12.6% | negative |
| **ROCE** | **~11.6%** | negative |
| PAT CAGR FY24–26 | **4.81%** | n/a (pre-scale) |
| IPO valuation | **~57× P/E**, mcap ₹6,244 cr | — |

**Two conclusions fall straight out of this table:**

1. **The prize is modest, even for the champion.** The *global market leader* in steroid APIs earns only ~**26% EBITDA / ~11–12% ROCE** and grew PAT at **<5%** — this is a **decent-margin but capital-heavy, slow-compounding, price-pressured** business. Winning steroid APIs does not produce Divi's/Concord-style economics; it produces pedestrian returns. So the *best realistic outcome* of Natural's ₹180 cr, debt-funded API bet is to become a small player in a business that pays low-teens ROCE — hardly a jackpot, and years away.

2. **The competitive gap is enormous.** Natural is a **sub-scale, loss-making new entrant** trying to take share in a market where one incumbent holds **38% global volume** and generates **₹869 cr** of API revenue. On cost, scale, approvals and customer relationships, Natural starts far behind.

**Higher-quality API/CDMO comps for context** (aspirational, not current peers): Concord Biotech, Divi's Laboratories, Laurus Labs, Cohance Lifesciences trade at premium multiples (~40–60× P/E) — but on the back of **30%+ EBITDA margins, high ROCE, and fermentation/CDMO moats** Natural's API does not have. The market pays up for *proven* API franchises; Natural is asking equity holders to fund an *unproven* one.

**Valuation implication:** You cannot value Natural's API on Symbiotec's 57× — that multiple belongs to a profitable global leader. Natural's API is worth, at best, invested capital adjusted for execution risk (i.e., a discount to the ~₹180 cr spent), and quite possibly less if breakeven slips. This **reinforces the SOTP conclusion**: the ₹223 cr mcap is not discounting API failure, so the risk/reward is skewed against the buyer.

**Net effect on verdict:** The peer cross-check makes the AVOID *more* confident, not less — the upside case (become a mid-tier steroid-API maker) is worth less than the market seems to assume, while the downside case (continued losses + dilution) is unchanged. **Conviction stays 3.5/10; the asymmetry is unfavourable.**
