## Executive Summary

STL (Sterlite Technologies) makes optical fibre preform, fibre, optical-fibre cable (OFC) and data-centre connectivity products. After demerging its loss-making Global Services business into STL Networks (effective 31 Mar 2025), it is now a focused optical company in the middle of the strongest demand upturn in its history. Q1 FY27 revenue was **₹1,910 Cr (+87% YoY)** with an EBITDA margin of **20.8%** and record PAT of ₹197 Cr. The order book rose **2.4× in one quarter to ₹18,618 Cr**. A ₹1,500 Cr QIP made the company **net-cash (₹483 Cr)**. Since then STL has announced three more hyperscaler/telecom supply agreements and a **$1.2 bn LTSA** (1 Oct 2026). In September it unveiled a "Lakshya" target of **₹20,000 Cr revenue and >27% EBITDA margin by FY29**, backed by a ₹3,000 Cr plan to add ~50% capacity ([Q1 FY27 call](https://stl.tech/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Transcript-July-24-2026.pdf); [Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/news/sterlite-technologies-approves-3-000-crore-capex-to-boost-capacity-by-50-by-fy29-3280-PE1_COR); [DSIJ](https://insights.dsij.in/appmindshare/sterlite-technologies-shares-hit-5-upper-circuit-as-company-unveils-ambitious-fy29-roadmap-fiis-and-diis-increase-stake-59401)).

**The price has run ahead of the story.** The stock went from **₹176 (30 Mar 2026) to ₹980, 5.6× in six months** (52-week low ₹84.6). It trades at **213× TTM P/E, 21× book (100th percentile of its 10-year range) and ~60× EV/TTM EBITDA**. Every order announced since the demerger is a **framework / long-term supply agreement valued at "prevailing prices"**: 54% of the Q1 FY27 book and ~80% of today's tracked book. On firm orders alone, revenue cover is ~1.5×, not 3.3×. The current price is roughly **15× the FY29 EPS that Lakshya would produce if delivered in full and on time**, so the market is already paying for the target. Even our bull-case DCF (₹526–823) is below the price. Expected 2-year value is **₹782 (−20%)**, and upside/downside is **0.6:1**, which triggers the hard stop.

**What is genuinely good, and why this is still "watch, don't own":** cash conversion is strong (11-year cumulative CFO is 2.9× PAT), the balance sheet is now net cash, the business is vertically integrated and holds 785+ patents, and hyperscalers are signing multi-year supply deals, which is evidence of real supply tightness. These are reasons to follow the stock closely. They are not reasons to pay 21× book for a business whose 10-year median ROCE is 11%.

## Business Primer

STL makes the full optical chain: it converts silicon into ultra-pure glass preforms, draws them into optical fibre, builds fibre into cables (including high-fibre-count cables for data centres) and sells connectivity products (pre-terminated trunks, MMC assemblies, FTTH kits) that make the fibre usable on site. Its customers are global and Indian telcos, neutral fibre-network builders, government rural-broadband programmes (BharatNet, US BEAD) and, increasingly, hyperscale data-centre operators. North America was 54% of Q1 FY27 revenue, Europe 25% and the rest of the world 22%. Data centres went from ~1% of FY26 revenue to 21% in Q1 FY27, and management expects data centre plus enterprise to reach ~50% in FY27. Revenue is fibre-km volume × price plus a rising "attach rate" of connectivity products (16% of OFC revenue, targeted at 25% by Q4 FY27). Returns depend on two things: keeping very capital-intensive plants full, and global fibre/cable prices, which have historically swung with Chinese capacity.

This is an **asset-heavy, vertically integrated manufacturer of a historically cyclical commodity, now riding a structural (AI data-centre) plus cyclical (supply shortage) upswing**. The key value drivers are volume × price and utilisation. The key risks are the fibre price cycle, concentration in a few hyperscaler customers, raw-material availability (germanium, helium) and US trade policy. One structural feature colours everything. The listed entity has been reshaped twice: power transmission was demerged in FY16 and Global Services in FY25. Long-run financials therefore mix businesses, and only FY24 onward is the "clean" optical company. The equity base has also been diluted by two QIPs (2024 and 2026) and promoter warrants priced at ₹110 against today's ₹980.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds

**Secular:** AI data centres (fibre per rack and per campus rising sharply as GPU links move from 400G to 800G and 1.6T), FTTH (US BEAD, BharatNet), 5G densification. Management cites CRU: global optical-cable demand +8.2% in 2026, data-centre cable demand +63% in 2026, North America 18.6% CAGR to 2030 ([Q1 FY27 call](https://stl.tech/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Transcript-July-24-2026.pdf)). These are real and multi-year.

**Cyclical:** Optical fibre is a classic capital-cycle industry. Prices spiked in 2017–18 (China shortage), collapsed in 2019–23 (STL's ROCE fell from 30% to 3%) and are tight again in 2025–26. The +87% Q1 growth is **partly secular (data-centre mix, attach rate) and partly cyclical** (utilisation recovering from a weak base and tight supply: "demand now consistently outpacing domestic supply in North America", per the call). Management also said it now has to "pick and choose" orders on capacity, which is a classic late-upcycle signal.

**Supply response is already under way:** STL plans +50% capacity by FY29 (₹3,000 Cr) plus ₹500 Cr/yr of upgrades and debottlenecking, and a $100 mn US connectivity plant. HFCL is expanding fibre from 28 to 34 mn fkm and OFC from 34 to 43 mn fkm. Corning, Prysmian and YOFC are adding AI-fibre capacity. **CY5 (industry capacity vs demand) and CY6 (fibre price cycle) are not charted:** no sourced public time series of fibre-km capacity, demand or price was found, and charts are never built from estimates. ## Module 2 — Industry Structure & Capital Cycle

**Five Forces:** entry barriers **moderate-high** (preform technology and scale; only a handful of integrated players outside China), supplier power **rising** (germanium and helium are concentrated and price-volatile), buyer power **high** (hyperscalers and telcos buy on allocation contracts with capped penalties and tender competitively), substitutes **low** (copper loses at 800G+; fibre is the only medium), rivalry **high but currently muted by shortage** (Corning, Prysmian, Fujikura, YOFC, HFCL). **Verdict: Neutral.** Demand is excellent, but structure caps through-cycle returns. STL's 10-year median ROCE of 11% is below a ~12% WACC.

**Capital cycle:** STL's capex/depreciation was 1.8, 1.3, 2.5, 4.3, 1.4, 1.6, 2.2, 1.2, 0.9, 0.4, 0.6× over FY16–FY26, a disciplined trough since FY23. It now steps up to ~2–3× (₹3,000 Cr over three years against ~₹320 Cr/yr depreciation). Peers are expanding at the same time, and sector equity windows are open (STL QIP ₹1,500 Cr at ₹583 in July 2026). **Signal: capital flooding in. Returns are excellent for now, with a deterioration phase likely in 2–3 years when FY28–FY29 capacity lands.** *(CY8 industry chart not built: peer capex series not compiled.)*

## Module 9 — Valuation: PIE First, Then DCF

**CMP ₹980 (9 Oct 2026, Screener) · Mcap ₹50,378 Cr · net cash ₹483 Cr (Jun 2026) · EV ~₹49,900 Cr · TTM EPS ₹4.98 · P/E 213× (Screener) · P/B 21.1× (BV ₹46.5) · EV/TTM EBITDA ~60× · EV/TTM revenue 8.8×. Diluted shares incl. promoter warrants: ~55.9 Cr.**

### PIE (reverse DCF)
Quick form: g = (EV × 12% − NOPAT₀)/(EV + NOPAT₀) = (49,895 × 0.12 − 435)/(49,895 + 435) = **11.0% perpetual NOPAT growth** from a TTM base that is already rising fast. That is essentially WACC: the price capitalises growth at the cost of capital forever. Two-stage version: to earn 12% from here, FY31 equity value must be ~₹88,000 Cr. At a mature multiple of ~12–20× NOPAT, that needs **FY31 NOPAT of ~₹4,400–7,500 Cr**, i.e. **revenue of ~₹29,000–50,000 Cr** at a 15% NOPAT margin (≈ 24–25% EBITDA).

| PIE driver | Market-implied | My base case | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue FY29 | ≈ Lakshya ₹20,000 Cr and growing beyond | ₹12,500 Cr | **Market far more optimistic** |
| Steady-state EBITDA margin | 25–27% (Lakshya) | 21% FY29 → 18% through-cycle | **Market optimistic** |
| Investment rate | Capacity +50% delivers 4× revenue | +50% capacity + price/mix ≈ 2.6× revenue | Market pricing unannounced capacity or permanent price gains |

**Physical plausibility check:** Today's capacity at full utilisation and today's prices supports ~₹10,900 Cr. With +50% capacity by FY29, the ceiling is ~₹14,700–16,400 Cr. **The PIE-implied ₹20,000 Cr+ requires either unannounced capacity beyond the ₹3,000 Cr plan or a further ~25–35% rise in realisation and mix.** Our view is that partial delivery (~₹12,500 Cr) is the realistic base: capacity ramps take 1–2 years, and the WTT history argues for a haircut.

**Expectations treadmill:** To beat the price, STL must deliver Lakshya in full and on time (₹20,000 Cr, >27% margin, FY29), *and* the market must still pay ~15× for it at a cyclical peak. **Positive triggers:** Q2 FY27 revenue ≥ ₹2,200 Cr at ≥ 22% margin; another firm (not framework) hyperscaler PO; early commissioning of expansion phases; attach rate ≥ 20% in Q2. **Negative triggers:** any quarter-on-quarter revenue dip; germanium/helium shortfall; hyperscaler capex cut; competitor capacity announcements; further equity issuance.
**SVAR:** bear-case price ₹145 → **SVAR 85%** (> 40% = unfavourable asymmetry).
