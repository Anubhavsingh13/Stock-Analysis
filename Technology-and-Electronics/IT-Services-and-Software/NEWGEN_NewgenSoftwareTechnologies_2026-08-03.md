## Executive Summary
Newgen is a debt-free, capital-light Indian software **product** company (low-code platform for content management, process automation, and customer communications, sold mainly to global BFSI and government) with a genuine moat from switching costs, 25% ROCE, ~20% ROE, and a recurring-revenue base that has risen to 62% of sales. The thesis: after a brutal ~47% price crash driven by a sharp FY26 growth deceleration (revenue +6% vs +19% in FY25) and heavy FII selling, the multiple has reset from ~45x to ~25x, and a return to low-teens AI/SaaS-led growth would deliver both earnings and re-rating upside. The key risk is that the deceleration is structural, not seasonal — management pointedly **withheld FY27 guidance** citing Middle East and large-deal timing uncertainty, and the stock remains in a technical downtrend (below its 200-DMA, making lower highs). Quality is not in question; timing and margin of safety are.

## BUSINESS PRIMER

Newgen Software makes and sells enterprise software products, not services. Its flagship is the **NewgenONE** low-code digital transformation platform, built around three long-standing engines: Enterprise Content Management (ECM), Business Process Management (BPM/workflow automation), and Customer Communication Management (CCM). Customers are almost entirely large institutions — banks, insurers, NBFCs, and government/PSU bodies — across India, the Americas, EMEA (with meaningful Middle East exposure), and APAC. Revenue flows through four buckets that are steadily shifting toward recurring: one-time license, implementation/services, annual support & maintenance (ATS/AMC), and the fast-growing subscription/SaaS (cloud) line. The economics depend on two things: (1) winning new enterprise logos and large platform deals, and (2) converting and retaining them onto annuity contracts that compound. Because it is a product company, incremental revenue carries high gross margin and needs little physical capital — growth is funded by people and R&D, not plant.

This is a **capital-light software compounder** with a **switching-cost moat**: once a bank runs its lending or content workflows on NewgenONE, replacement is expensive and risky, giving Newgen pricing power and high retention. The key value drivers are revenue growth (new deals + annuity expansion) and operating margin (operating leverage as subscription scales); the key risks are (a) lumpy, back-ended license revenue that makes any single quarter noisy — Q4 is seasonally huge, Q1 seasonally weak; (b) customer/geography concentration in BFSI and the Middle East; and (c) a large and growing treasury (~₹700 Cr of investments) that inflates "other income," distorts reported PAT versus operating performance, and — because payout is only ~24% — drags ROE. Every module below is read through the fact that operating performance and *reported* PAT can diverge sharply in any given year.

**New Segment Protocol:** Primary segment = **IT / Enterprise Software (product)**, adjacent to the covered "IT Services" segment. KPI reference is available and applied with product-specific adaptation (annuity mix, SaaS/ARR growth, order book/ACV, net-new logos in place of pure headcount/utilisation metrics). No pause required — segment is effectively covered ✓.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds
**Sector: Enterprise software / digital transformation for BFSI + government.**

**Secular vs. cyclical decomposition (critical step):**
- **Secular (durable):** BFSI digitisation, cloud/SaaS migration, low-code adoption, AI-enablement of workflows, formalisation of government processes. This supports a **structural ~12–15% growth baseline** and is the anchor for DCF mid-period growth.
