## Business Primer

Urban Company is India's largest full-stack online marketplace for **home and beauty services** — cleaning, plumbing, electrical, appliance repair, salon-at-home, massage — across 59 cities (48 in India plus UAE, Singapore, KSA). "Full-stack" means it doesn't just list professionals: it trains and equips them, sets prices and quality standards, owns the customer relationship, and monetises at a high **take rate (~36% of NTV)** via commission plus product/consumable sales and own-brand products (Native water purifiers). In FY26 it ran **₹4,290 Cr of Net Transaction Value (NTV, +33%)** through **8.4 M annual transacting users (+24%)** and **~59,500 monthly active service partners**, converting to **₹1,556 Cr revenue (+36%)**. 83% of NTV came from repeat users — strong stickiness. It is a two-sided network: value compounds as more users attract more partners and vice-versa, with brand, quality-control, and supply-training as the differentiators.

This is a **capital-light consumer-internet platform**, and the defining feature is that its economics are **two businesses in one**. The **mature portfolio — India core services, international, and Native — is profitable** (India core earned a ₹82 Cr segment profit in Q1FY27; adjusted EBITDA ex-new-bets was +₹106 Cr in FY26 and core adj-EBITDA rose to 6.9% of NTV). The **entire consolidated loss is one deliberate blitzscale bet: InstaHelp** — 15-minute on-demand domestic help/housekeeping — which lost **₹131.6 Cr in Q1FY27** on ₹11 Cr revenue, and which management is *intentionally* keeping in heavy-burn mode to grab scale (it crossed 100,000 daily orders in Aug 2026; UBS calls it a possible "Blinkit moment"). So the headline is ugly (FY26 net loss ₹235 Cr; FY25's ₹240 Cr "profit" was a one-time deferred-tax credit, not operations), but it masks a profitable, growing core funding a high-risk land-grab. The key value driver is the durable, high-retention marketplace; the key risk is **capital allocation** — whether InstaHelp becomes a second engine or an open-ended cash sink — layered on a **full valuation** (~5.2× trailing NTV) and a low ~19% founder/promoter holding (normal for VC-funded firms).
