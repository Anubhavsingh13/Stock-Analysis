## Executive Summary
Gandhar is a ₹2,246 Cr specialty-oils processor (white oils, healthcare/consumer specialty oils, automotive & transformer oils) that has traded at a persistently low multiple because it is fundamentally a **thin-margin, spread-driven commodity converter** with ~flat revenue for five years. The stock has just spiked to a 52-week high on a spectacular Q1 FY27 (revenue +92% YoY, PAT +689% YoY, OPM 16.2% vs a normal 4–6%). The central question is whether that quarter is a durable step-up from the pharma/healthcare specialty mix shift, or a base-oil spread/inventory windfall that mean-reverts. The headline ~7× P/E looks cheap, but on **normalised** commodity-cycle earnings it is not obviously so, and the low quality of the underlying business argues against extrapolating one blowout quarter.

## Business Primer
Gandhar Oil Refinery makes petroleum-based specialty products under the "Divyol" brand: white oils and liquid paraffins (for pharma, personal care, food-grade uses), industrial/process oils, transformer oils, automotive oils, and a consumer/healthcare (Oleopharma) portfolio. It buys base oil, processes/blends it, and sells value-added specialty grades — so earnings are essentially a **processing spread** (product realisation minus base-oil cost) times volume, across India and international operations (including a UAE/Suez presence). Revenue has hovered around ₹4,000 Cr for years; the swing factor in any given period is the spread, which tracks crude/base-oil price moves and product-mix.

This is a **commodity/specialty converter** — closer to a low-margin manufacturer than a branded consumer compounder. The value driver is spread × volume × mix; the key risk is that margins are structurally thin (4–8% OPM historically) and cyclical, with meaningful working-capital and FX exposure. The structural feature colouring the whole analysis: **reported profit is highly sensitive to base-oil price cycles and inventory timing**, so a single quarter — even a record one — is weak evidence of a permanent re-rating.
