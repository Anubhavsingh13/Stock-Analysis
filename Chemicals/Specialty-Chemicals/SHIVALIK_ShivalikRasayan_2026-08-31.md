## Executive Summary
Shivalik Rasayan is a small-cap that has transformed from a cash-generative, high-ROCE agrochemical maker (Dimethoate/Malathion) into a capital-hungry, low-ROCE pharma-API conglomerate that consolidates the separately-listed Medicamen Biotech (~40% owned). Revenue tripled over a decade (₹198→₹368 Cr consolidated) but consolidated profit **fell** from a ₹34 Cr peak (FY22) to ₹18 Cr (FY26), ROCE collapsed from 32% to 5%, and operating cash flow has been **negative three years running** while working capital ballooned (cash-conversion cycle 244 days). At ~42× consolidated owner-earnings the stock is pricing a full recovery that its USFDA-approved oncology-API plant *might* eventually deliver — but the price offers no margin of safety for the very real execution, working-capital, and dilution risks. **The thesis is a possible earnings-recovery option bought at an expensive strike with poor cash-flow quality; wait for evidence, not hope.**

## Business Primer

Shivalik Rasayan Ltd (SRL) makes money three ways. First, its legacy engine is **agrochemicals** — it is India's largest producer of Dimethoate Technical and second-largest of Malathion Technical, organophosphorus insecticides sold B2B to formulators and exporters, where economics are volume × realization on commodity-ish molecules. Second, it has built a **greenfield API (active pharmaceutical ingredient) business** at Dahej, Gujarat — a state-of-the-art oncology and general-API plant designed to USFDA/EDQM standards, which cleared USFDA inspection in 2024 and is now in ramp-up. Third, and most importantly for the reported numbers, SRL controls and **consolidates Medicamen Biotech** (a separately BSE/NSE-listed finished-dosage and API company) despite owning only ~40% of it — so roughly half of consolidated revenue and a chunk of profit belong economically to *other* shareholders, showing up as minority interest. Revenue therefore flows from three different engines with three different return profiles, and the consolidated statements blend them.

This is best classified as a **capital-heavy, transitioning specialty-chemical/pharma business with a holding-company-like overlay** — not a clean compounder. The key value driver going forward is **margin and asset-turn recovery** as the new API capacity fills; the key risks are stacked: (1) a multi-year capex binge that has driven ROCE from 32% to 5% and turned operating cash flow negative, (2) extreme working-capital intensity (inventory 285 days, receivables 114 days), (3) a complex consolidation of a 40%-owned listed entity that inflates the balance sheet with minority interest and makes "look-through" ownership economics opaque, and (4) an ongoing preferential share-plus-warrant issue that dilutes existing holders. The entire bull case rests on the unproven proposition that the API plant ramps to high utilization at good margins fast enough to justify a valuation already priced for success.

### 1c — Stock Cycle: Company-Specific Positioning
- **Revenue trajectory:** Rising in absolute terms but **profit decelerating/declining** — classic late-capex, pre-payoff phase.
- **Volume/price/mix:** Growth is capacity-(volume)-led with adverse price/mix in agrochem; API mix improvement is the hoped-for future driver, not yet in the numbers.
- **Capacity model:** Major greenfield API plant commissioned FY24–FY25; standard greenfield ramp = 25–35% Yr1 → 60–70% Yr2 → 85–90% Yr3. SRL is early on this curve → **margins structurally suppressed until utilization builds**.

- **FCF inflection:** **Not yet reached.** OCF negative; FCF deeply negative (−₹63 Cr, −₹22 Cr, −₹13 Cr FY24–26 standalone). Inflection depends entirely on capex ending *and* working capital normalizing.
- **Classification: Early-cycle / Ramp-up** — but *without* the margin recovery yet visible, and bought at a late-cycle price.
