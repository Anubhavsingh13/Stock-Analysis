## BUSINESS PRIMER

Sunshield Chemicals manufactures **surfactants and specialty functional chemicals** — principally ethoxylates and propoxylates, plus niche molecules like THEIC and HQEE (polymer/PU additives), antioxidants, and urea-formaldehyde resin. Revenue is **B2B, volume × realization**, sold to formulators across Home & Personal Care, Agrochemicals, Paints & Coatings, and Industrial Formulations. Customers include blue-chip names (BASF, Asian Paints, Hindustan Petroleum). Operations are a single manufacturing complex in Maharashtra (Roha/Mahad belt). The economics depend on two things: **ethylene-oxide-derivative feedstock spreads** (the raw material is crude/petrochemical-linked) and **customer stickiness for customized/tailor-made grades**, where Sunshield positions itself as a formulation partner rather than a bulk commodity supplier. The company was historically a BASF subsidiary; **BASF has since exited and is now a customer**, and Indus Petrochem Ltd acquired 62.36% in Nov-2021, becoming the strategic promoter.

This is a **specialty-chemicals processor sitting between commodity and true-specialty** — closer to the commodity end than premium players like Fine Organics or Galaxy Surfactants. The key value driver is **volume growth + mix upgrade** (moving from tolling/bulk ethoxylates toward higher-margin customized and export grades); the key risks are **input-cost cyclicality** (ethylene oxide / crude), **customer concentration** in a B2B book, and **microcap-specific risks** — a ~88 lakh share float, thin liquidity, and sparse disclosure. Two structural features colour the entire analysis: (1) the business only became durably profitable from FY21, so the "10-year record" is really a **5-year turnaround record**, and (2) the strong, chemicals-native promoter (Indus Petrochem) that is *creeping its stake up* (64.5%→66.5% over 3 quarters) on a small float creates latent **delisting/consolidation optionality** — a catalyst and a governance consideration both.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Specialty Chemicals — Tailwind (Moderating)

**Secular vs. cyclical decomposition of Sunshield's growth is the single most important read here.** FY26 revenue grew 20.5% and 5-yr sales CAGR is 17%, but this blends:
- **Secular (durable, ~8–10%):** China+1 sourcing shift, formalization, HPC/agrochem demand, mix upgrade to customized grades and exports.
- **Cyclical (temporary, ~7–10% of recent growth):** Post-destocking restocking in global specialty chemicals (FY24–FY26 recovery off the FY23 inventory correction) and a favourable input-cost window (softer crude/EO passing through as margin, OPM 9%→12%).

**Do NOT embed 17–20% as permanent.** The durable baseline that should anchor a DCF mid-period is **~10–12%**. Reported FY26/Q1FY27 acceleration is partly the sector cycle turning up after the FY23–FY24 global chemicals downcycle — the same restock that has lifted Galaxy Surfactants, Fine Organics and Aarti Surfactants.

**Input-cost cycle:** Ethylene oxide / crude-linked — currently benign, hence the margin expansion. **Rising crude would compress margins** given limited pricing power at the commodity end. **Competitive capacity:** the Indian surfactant/ethoxylate space is adding capacity across multiple players (Galaxy, Aarti Surfactants, Fine Organics, Godrej Industries) — **simultaneous additions risk pricing pressure** as the cycle matures. **Sector verdict: Tailwind, but moderating and cyclically extended** — treat FY26 margins as near cycle-peak, not a new baseline.
