## BUSINESS PRIMER

Lumino Industries makes money two ways that feed each other. The larger engine (~70% of FY26 revenue, ₹1,423 cr) is **manufacturing**: it produces aluminium conductors — including high-temperature low-sag (HTLS) conductors used to re-string existing transmission lines at higher capacity — plus power cables, railway signalling cables and house wires (brand "Lumicon"), out of two plants in Howrah, West Bengal. The smaller engine (~30%, ₹618 cr) is **EPC**: turnkey power transmission and distribution projects, EHV substations up to 400 kV, HTLS re-conductoring, railway electrification, solar and water-management projects. Revenue is essentially **volume × realization on a commodity input (aluminium/copper/steel = ~84% of costs)** for the manufacturing arm, and **project execution against a tendered order book** for EPC. The two arms interlock: ~23% of the specialised products consumed in its own EPC projects are made in-house, giving captive demand and a bid advantage. Customers are overwhelmingly government — State Electricity Boards, PGCIL, railways — making this a B2B/B2G business where **collections, not sales, are the binding constraint.**

This is a **cyclical, asset-and-working-capital-heavy industrial** riding a real secular tailwind (India's ₹9–10 lakh crore transmission capex plan through 2032, RDSS distribution upgrades, grid expansion for renewables). Its key value drivers are order-book conversion and margin mix (a strategic tilt toward higher-value EHV substations, now 44.6% of the EPC book, is the margin story). The key risks are structural and specific: **(1) working-capital intensity** — long project cycles, milestone billing and 5–10% retention money against government buyers who pay slowly; **(2) commodity pass-through risk** on aluminium/copper; and **(3) single-geography concentration** (all plants in Howrah). The colouring feature for the entire analysis is that reported ROE (24.6%) and PAT are of **low cash quality historically** and are **leverage-flattered** — post-IPO the equity base roughly doubles and debt is repaid, so ROE will structurally step down to the mid-teens even if operations hold. Read every profit number in this report through the cash-conversion lens.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds

**Secular vs cyclical decomposition.** Lumino's FY24→FY26 revenue CAGR of ~20% (and FY23→FY24 near-doubling) blends a **durable secular baseline of ~12–14%** (grid expansion, RDSS, renewable evacuation, HTLS re-conductoring — all multi-year, policy-backed) with a **cyclical surge of ~6–8%** from the current synchronized T&D capex upcycle and post-tender-award execution bunching. **Do not embed 20%+ as permanent.** The DCF mid-period anchor should be ~12–13%.

- **Input cost cycle:** Aluminium/copper elevated and volatile; 84% of costs are raw material. Conductor realizations are largely pass-through with a lag → margin compresses when metals spike mid-contract. Use current (not peak) margin.
- **Competitive capacity:** Multiple listed conductor/EPC players (Apar, KEC, Kalpataru, Bajel, Skipper) are all expanding into the same T&D window → risk of tender price competition. Structurally this caps EPC margins in single digits.
- **Geopolitical/FX:** Primarily domestic; limited direct FX exposure. Aluminium is globally priced (LME) — an indirect input-cost channel.
- **Sector cycle verdict: TAILWIND (secular, multi-year), with a cyclical overlay and margin-competition caveat.** Primary driver: India transmission capex super-cycle to 2032. Duration: structural, but current order-inflow intensity is partly cyclical.
- **Downstream:** Do not embed cyclical surge as permanent (Module 8). Sector is favourable, so no extra MoS for the sector — but company-specific WC risk drives the MoS instead.
