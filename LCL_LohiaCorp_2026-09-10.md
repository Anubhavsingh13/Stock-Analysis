## Executive Summary
Lohia Corp is the **India #1 (≈40.7% value share) and global #2 (≈15.4%) maker of woven-raffia machinery** — the tape-extrusion, circular-loom, coating and printing lines that convert polypropylene into woven sacks and technical fabrics. It is a genuinely high-quality capital-goods franchise: **ROCE ~44%, ROE ~45%, negative-ish working capital (customer advances fund it), cash conversion above 1.0× (FY26 CFO ₹325 cr vs PAT ₹193 cr), falling debt, and 25%+ revenue growth with 71% TTM profit growth.** At ~₹545 (P/E ~24–30 trailing, ~22× forward on the Q1FY27 run-rate) for a dominant, cash-generative global #2 growing at 25%, the valuation is *reasonable*, not stretched — a rarity in the 2026 IPO cohort. The two real caveats: (1) it's a **100% OFS** — no growth capital raised, the Lohia family monetised ₹1,101 cr; and (2) it is a **cyclical machinery maker with 88% of revenue from a single segment** riding a customer-capex upcycle, so the 71% profit surge is partly cyclical and the multiple should be judged on mid-cycle, not peak, earnings. **Best-quality name of this four; own it on any market/cyclical pullback.**

## Business Primer
Lohia Corp makes the *machines that make woven plastic*. Its core product is the complete line for producing woven-raffia (woven polypropylene/HDPE) fabric: tape-extrusion plants that draw PP granules into flat tapes, circular looms that weave those tapes into tubular fabric, and the downstream coating, lamination, printing, bag-conversion, multifilament-yarn and plastic-recycling equipment. Customers are manufacturers of woven sacks, FIBC/bulk bags, agro-textiles, geotextiles and flexible packaging across India and ~100 export markets. Revenue is essentially **machinery sales (project-like, order-driven) plus spares/service** — sold with customer advances, which is why its working capital is near zero. ~88% of FY26 revenue is the woven-raffia machinery segment; it is backward-integrated (makes key sub-assemblies in-house) from its Kanpur base.

This is a **cyclical, asset-moderate capital-goods franchise with a strong local moat**. The key value driver is **order inflow tied to downstream packaging/woven-sack capex** and the aftermarket (spares/service) annuity; the key risks are **cyclicality** (machine demand swings with customers' willingness to invest, which tracks packaging demand, PP prices and credit availability), **single-segment concentration** (raffia machinery is ~88% of revenue), steel/component input costs, and competition from established European majors (Windmöller & Hölscher, Starlinger). A structural feature to keep front-of-mind: the current 44% ROE and 71% profit jump reflect a **cyclical up-leg** in customer capex — the franchise is excellent, but the reported returns are near a cyclical high and will normalise.
