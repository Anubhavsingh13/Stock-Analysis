## Business Primer
Trishakti Industries (incorporated 1985, Kolkata) rents heavy infrastructure equipment — crawler cranes, truck cranes, piling rigs and grabs — to EPC, railway, steel and power project contractors. Revenue is essentially a rental/lease spread: it buys capital equipment, funds it with debt, and earns hire charges over the asset life. It has recently described itself as moving to a "pure-play asset-leasing" model and has partnered with the MYCRANE digital rental platform. The economics depend entirely on two things: keeping expensive cranes utilised at good day-rates, and collecting from contractor/government-linked clients on time.

This is an **asset-heavy, capital-cycle-driven, working-capital-intensive** business with no evident moat. The key value driver is asset utilisation × realisation; the key risks are leverage (debt funds every incremental crane), receivables (contractor payment cycles are long — already 200+ days here), and cyclicality tied to the infrastructure capex cycle. The dominant structural feature colouring the entire analysis is that this is a **tiny, recently-transformed entity with a very short and volatile operating record**, so almost none of the historical financials describe the business as it exists today. The listed crane-rental benchmark in India is Sanghvi Movers — a far larger, established peer that frames what "good" looks like in this niche.

**Segment: Infrastructure Equipment Leasing / Crane Rental** — not in the standard KPI reference (closest analog: Infra/EPC + asset-leasing). KPIs that matter: fleet utilisation %, realisation/day-rate, debtor days, net debt/EBITDA, ROCE vs cost of debt.
