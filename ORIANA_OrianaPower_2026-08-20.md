## Executive Summary
Oriana Power is a hyper-growth renewable-energy platform (solar EPC + owned IPP/BOOT assets + an early green-hydrogen/ammonia ambition) that has compounded revenue at ~120% and profit at ~150% over five years, yet whose stock has collapsed ~60% from its ₹3,064 high to ₹1,212 and now trades at just 9.3× earnings. The market's 9× multiple is not mispricing quality — it is pricing the risk that this growth is cyclical/order-book-dependent, cash-poor, and dilution-prone. **The single most important disagreement to resolve is whether Oriana is an EPC contractor (deserves 8–12× and is fairly priced) or a compounding renewable asset owner (deserves a re-rating).**

## BUSINESS PRIMER

Oriana Power sells clean-energy projects and, increasingly, clean-energy *output*. Its core engine is solar EPC — it designs, procures and builds solar plants (835+ MWp commissioned, 700+ MWp pipeline) for commercial, industrial and government customers, earning project-based revenue (order value recognised as construction progresses). Alongside pure EPC, it builds and *keeps* some solar assets on a BOOT (Build-Own-Operate-Transfer) basis, selling the electricity/savings to an offtaker over 15–25 years — this converts one-time EPC revenue into an annuity but consumes capital up front. Layered on top is a far more ambitious and speculative third leg: integrated green hydrogen, green ammonia and green methanol (a secured 60,000 MTPA green-ammonia allocation, a ₹4,500 Cr Maharashtra MoU, and a stated 2,00,000 MTPA green-hydrogen target by FY28). Revenue today is overwhelmingly EPC (volume of projects × realisation), so the two things the business absolutely depends on are (a) a steady flow of new solar orders at workable margins, and (b) the ability to *finance* the BOOT/hydrogen assets without diluting shareholders or over-levering.

This is a **hybrid asset-heavy infrastructure/EPC business with a capital-cycle tailwind and a promoter-scale-ambition risk.** The key value driver is order inflow converted to executed revenue at 18–22% EBITDA margins; the key risks are the classic EPC afflictions — receivables stretch, execution/working-capital drag, margin compression as competition floods in, and the temptation to fund grand IPP/hydrogen visions with debt and equity the balance sheet cannot yet support. A structural feature that colours the entire analysis: the company is simultaneously trying to be three different businesses (a capital-light EPC contractor, a capital-heavy power producer, and a moonshot green-molecule developer) with very different economics and return profiles — and the market is refusing to pay for the ambition until cash generation proves it.

**Segment classification: Power & Utilities + Infrastructure/EPC — both covered in the KPI reference ✓**
