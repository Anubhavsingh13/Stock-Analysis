## Executive Summary
Borosil Renewables is India's first and largest solar (photovoltaic) glass manufacturer, now a pure-play Indian franchise after formally exiting its loss-making German subsidiary (GMB) via insolvency in July 2025. The thesis is a genuine, already-in-progress turnaround: anti-dumping + countervailing duties on Chinese, Vietnamese and Malaysian solar glass (imposed Dec-2024 to Jun-2026) lifted domestic realizations from ₹138/sq.m (Q1FY26) to ₹160/sq.m (Q1FY27), taking EBITDA margins from ~4% (FY24–25) to a sustained 31–35% (last four quarters). The single biggest risk is the **capital cycle**: Indian industry solar glass capacity is set to more than triple from ~2,650 TPD to ~9,350 TPD (~60 GW) by FY27 as Vishakha, Triveni, Gold Plus, Saint-Gobain and others expand simultaneously — a classic setup for pricing/margin normalisation off today's policy-protected peak, even though ~75% of Indian demand is still import-supplied.

## BUSINESS PRIMER

Borosil Renewables makes low-iron, textured, tempered "solar glass" — the transparent front cover that protects a photovoltaic module and lets sunlight through with minimal loss. It is a B2B commodity-like industrial product sold to solar module manufacturers (Waaree, Premier Energies, Adani, Tata Power, Vikram Solar and dozens of smaller cell/module lines). Revenue is fundamentally **volume × realization**: production is measured in tonnes-per-day (TPD) of furnace capacity and sold per square metre of finished glass. As of Q1FY27 the company runs 1,000 TPD (~6.5 GW of module-equivalent glass) at 100% utilisation, producing ~102.5 million sq.m/year at a realization of ₹160.30/sq.m (Q1FY27 concall). The two things the business absolutely depends on are (a) a protected domestic price for solar glass — currently underpinned by anti-dumping/countervailing duties against China, Vietnam and Malaysia — and (b) cheap, uninterrupted energy (natural gas for the furnace plus captive solar-wind power), because glass melting is extremely energy-intensive and the furnaces must run continuously.

This is an **asset-heavy, commodity manufacturer in a major capex/expansion cycle**, and until mid-2025 it was also burdened by a structurally loss-making European operation which has now been shed. The key value drivers are all three of price (realization), volume (capacity + utilisation) and margin (operating leverage + energy cost), with price currently doing the heavy lifting. The dominant risk is the **commodity + capital cycle**: solar glass is globally deflationary (China exports at scale), so Borosil's healthy current economics rest on a policy wall (import duties) and on domestic supply meeting only ~25% of Indian demand. Two structural features colour the entire analysis: first, the FY26 numbers are distorted by a one-time ~₹272 crore German write-off in Q1FY26 (masking an otherwise strongly profitable year); second, the balance sheet was recapitalised in FY25–26 via preferential issues (~₹235 Cr in Feb-2025 and ~₹371 Cr in Oct-2025), leaving the company near net-cash and fully funding the ₹950 Cr expansion. Every module below is read through the lens of "the turnaround is real, but is it priced, and is the margin peak durable?"

### 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds
**Secular vs. Cyclical demand decomposition (critical step):**
- **Secular (durable):** India's solar module capacity build-out under ALMM/PLI and China+1; domestic solar glass supplies only ~25% of Indian demand — a genuine multi-year import-substitution runway (~75% still imported per Q1FY27 concall). This supports **volume** growth structurally.
- **Cyclical / policy-dependent (mean-reverting):** The 31–35% EBITDA margin and ₹160/sq.m realization are **not** a secular baseline — they are a function of the anti-dumping duty wall and a temporary domestic supply deficit. Global solar glass pricing is structurally deflationary.

**Verdict on the growth mix:** The *volume* growth is largely secular; the *margin/realization* level is largely cyclical/policy-driven. **Do NOT embed 31–35% EBITDA margins as permanent** in the DCF. Durable normalized margin is more likely 22–26%; durable volume growth 12–18% until the capacity ceiling.

**Input cost cycle:** Natural gas is the swing cost. West Asia conflict pushed energy up in early 2026, prompting a ₹9.50/sq.m fuel surcharge from Mar-2026. Captive solar-wind hybrid now supplies 93% of power needs (~₹18 Cr/yr savings). Energy cost = the primary margin sensitivity → **Neutral-to-adverse**, model margin compression risk.

**Competitive capacity pipeline (the decisive signal):** This is a textbook **simultaneous multi-player capacity flood**. Industry capacity is set to rise from ~2,650 TPD to **~9,350 TPD (~60 GW) by FY27** — Vishakha (→1,260 TPD), Triveni (850), Indosolar (1,000), Gold Plus (~2,050+), Saint-Gobain (450), GREW (300), plus Borosil's own +600. Sources: pv-magazine India, glass-international, AIGMF. Multiple players adding capacity in the same 12–24 month window → structural oversupply risk → pricing pressure regardless of demand, *unless* Indian module demand grows fast enough to absorb it.

**Sector cycle verdict: TAILWIND ON VOLUME / HEADWIND ON PRICING.** Primary driver: policy-protected import substitution (multi-year) vs. domestic oversupply wave (12–24 months). Net: treat current margins as a cyclical peak; require a wider margin of safety (Module 8 applies +5–10pp wider MoS).

### 1c — Stock Cycle: Company-Specific Positioning
**Revenue growth trajectory (quarterly YoY):** Accelerating sharply on price — Sales Q1FY26 ₹347 Cr → Q1FY27 ₹406 Cr (+17%), but the swing is margin, not just volume (volume +8% YoY per concall).
**Revenue decomposition:** Recent CAGR is **price/realization-led** (most fragile driver) with modest volume. Forward volume growth comes from the +600 TPD hard-capacity add. Mix is improving (fuel surcharge pass-through).
**Physical capacity model:** 1,000 TPD now (100% utilised) → 1,600 TPD (~10.5 GW) by ~Dec-2026, commissioning Q4FY27, full revenue FY28. At 100% utilisation today, near-term volume growth is **capacity-locked** until SG-4/SG-5 ramp — so FY27 growth must come from price/mix, and the step-up is an FY28 event.
**Operating leverage:** High DOL (furnace fixed costs) — at 100% utilisation, margin is highly sensitive to realization. A ₹10/sq.m realization move (~6%) swings EBITDA materially.
**FCF inflection:** FY26 OCF ₹423 Cr but investing ₹-736 Cr (expansion + captive power + treasury) → FCF negative during build. FCF inflection likely **FY28**, once SG-4/SG-5 commissioned and capex drops to maintenance.
**Stock cycle classification: EARLY/MID-CYCLE RAMP with a policy-driven earnings surge** — capex-heavy, revenue accelerating, but margins at a cyclical peak rather than suppressed. Unusual hybrid: operationally early-cycle, but margin-wise late-cycle. MoS treatment: standard-to-wider.

## Module 3 — Business Quality & Moat

### Greenwald's Three Barriers
1. **Supply/Cost advantage:** Partial. Borosil has first-mover scale (India's largest, ~15%+ domestic share), captive solar-wind power (93% of needs), and process maturity. But a well-capitalised entrant *can* replicate the cost structure by spending ₹900–1,000 Cr — and several are. **Weak-to-moderate.**
2. **Customer captivity:** Low. Solar glass is a commodity; module makers multi-source and switch on price/quality. No meaningful switching costs. **Absent.**
3. **Local economies of scale:** Partial. Scale leadership in a bounded domestic market behind a duty wall — but the "wall" is policy, not a structural moat, and domestic share is being diluted by entrants. **Weak.**

**Moat Verdict: NARROW and policy-dependent.** The real "moat" today is the anti-dumping/countervailing duty regime plus first-mover scale — genuine but externally granted and time-limited (5-year duties). Buffett price test (raise 10% without losing volume): passes *only while imports are duty-blocked and domestic supply is short* — fails once domestic oversupply arrives.

### Moat Durability
- ROIC > WACC: Yes currently (ROIC ~25%+ vs WACC ~12%), but only 2 quarters into the up-cycle; historically ROIC was sub-WACC (FY24–25 losses).
- Market share stability: Falling (74% → lower as new domestic entrants take share of the growing pie).
- Pricing power: Present now, policy-derived, not structural.

## Module 7 — Market Size & Market Share

**TAM:** Indian solar glass demand is derived from domestic module manufacturing, itself scaling rapidly under ALMM/PLI toward ~100+ GW of module capacity by FY27. Domestic solar glass supply meets only **~25% of Indian demand** (Q1FY27 concall) — i.e., ~75% still imported. This is a large, growing, under-penetrated TAM.
**Penetration / share:** Borosil is the largest domestic producer (~15%+ of the domestic-supplied share), but domestic share is fragmenting as entrants arrive.
**Reconciliation:** Company volume +8% YoY (capacity-constrained at 100% utilisation) while the *market* is growing much faster — so Borosil is currently **growing slower than the market on volume** (capacity-locked) and gaining on *value* (price). Post-expansion (FY28) it re-accelerates on volume.
**Verdict:** Huge structural demand runway (bullish for volume), but the same runway is attracting a supply flood (bearish for price). The market is big enough that *volume* can grow for years; the open question is *at what margin.*
