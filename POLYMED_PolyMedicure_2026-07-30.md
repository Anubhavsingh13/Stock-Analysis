## Executive Summary
Poly Medicure is India's leading manufacturer of medical-device consumables (infusion therapy, renal/dialysis, oncology, anaesthesia, surgery, diagnostics), a genuine China+1 export franchise selling to 125+ countries with ~68% of revenue from exports, a debt-free balance sheet, and a promoter who *raised* his stake to 62.4% in FY25. The thesis is a multi-year global-share-gain and product-premiumisation story riding a structural medical-consumables tailwind. **The problem is price and momentum, not quality:** the stock trades at ~52× trailing earnings while FY26 earnings actually *fell* 2%, EBITDA margins compressed ~650 bps in Q4, ROCE has structurally eroded from 30% (FY15) to 15% (FY26), working-capital days have ballooned to 259, and FIIs have cut their stake from 14.5% to 5.6% over eight quarters. The single biggest risk is **multiple de-rating: a premium valuation resting on decelerating, margin-pressured, capital-intensity-rising earnings.** No margin of safety exists at the current price — this is a high-quality business to own materially lower.

## BUSINESS PRIMER

Poly Medicure makes single-use and semi-durable medical devices — the plastic-and-polymer "consumables" that hospitals burn through daily: IV cannulas, infusion sets, blood-management and transfusion products, dialysis (renal) consumables and machines, oncology/chemo delivery devices, anaesthesia and respiratory products, urology, gastroenterology and surgical disposables, and a growing high-complexity range (orthopaedics via Citieffe, cardiology, diagnostics). Revenue is overwhelmingly B2B — sold to hospitals, distributors, government tenders and OEM/private-label partners — and flows as **volume × realization**, so the economics depend on (1) low-cost, high-volume polymer manufacturing scale in India and (2) regulatory approvals and distribution reach that let it export. Exports are the engine: ~68% of FY26 revenue (₹1,280 Cr of ₹1,875 Cr) is international, spread across Europe, the US, MENA/West Asia, Africa and Asia, with domestic (~32%) growing faster (20% in FY26) than international (9%). The two things the business absolutely depends on are its **India cost advantage in medical plastics** and its **pipeline of regulatory approvals + new products** (35 launched in FY26, 20 developed in-house) that keep widening the addressable catalogue.

This is an **asset-heavy, export-oriented manufacturer with a genuine (but not impregnable) low-cost moat** — closest in character to a specialty-manufacturing compounder than to a branded-consumer business. The key value drivers are **volume growth (capacity-led) and gross-margin/mix premiumisation**; the key risks are **input-cost/polymer and freight cycles, FX (large net USD/EUR export exposure), Chinese import competition (acute in the renal segment), and rising capital intensity** as the company builds greenfield capacity and buys smaller overseas firms (Citieffe-Italy, PendraCare-Netherlands, Medyneo-Brazil). Two structural features colour the whole analysis: first, a large **treasury portfolio (~₹1,600 Cr of cash+investments)** raised via a FY25 equity issue — this inflates reported PAT with ~₹119 Cr of non-operating other income (27% of operating profit) and simultaneously depresses ROE; second, the company is **deep in a capex/M&A cycle**, so trailing returns understate steady-state economics but current earnings are being pressured by ramp costs and integration.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds
**Verdict: Structural Tailwind, with a near-term cyclical margin headwind.**

**Secular vs cyclical decomposition of growth:** Of Poly Medicure's ~16–19% multi-year revenue CAGR, the **durable secular baseline is ~12–14%** — driven by (a) global medical-consumables demand growth (5–7%), (b) India-based China+1 share gains, (c) rising domestic healthcare penetration, and (d) new-product/geography expansion. The **cyclical/transient component** is modest (a few points from acquisitions and post-COVID normalisation). Anchor DCF mid-period growth at ~12–13%, **not** the 20%+ headline the market appears to be paying for.

- **Input-cost cycle:** Rising — polymer/resin and freight (West Asia/Red Sea logistics disruption explicitly cited by management) compressed FY26 margins. Treat as *partly transient* (freight re-routable) but *partly structural* (competitive intensity limits pass-through). → compress near-term margin assumptions.
- **Competitive capacity pipeline:** Chinese renal/consumable exporters are adding low-cost capacity — a structural oversupply/pricing risk in commoditised sub-segments (dialysis).
- **Net FX exposure:** ~68% revenue in foreign currency vs a smaller foreign cost base → **large net long USD/EUR position (~50%+ net).** A 5% INR appreciation would be a material EBIT headwind; conversely INR depreciation is a tailwind. High FX sensitivity is a permanent feature.
- **Geopolitical:** West Asia logistics (Red Sea) = transient-to-semi-structural freight cost; sourcing largely India-based (lower China API-type dependence than pharma).

**Downstream effect:** Do NOT embed 20%+ as permanent DCF growth. Sector margin headwind + FX risk → widen required MoS by 5–10 pp.

## Module 1c — Stock Cycle: Company-Specific Positioning
**Classification: Late-early / mid-cycle capex-heavy phase with DECELERATING earnings momentum — a warning even though absolute growth is positive.**

**Revenue growth trajectory (YoY, consol quarters):** Dec-24 +? , Mar-25, then Jun-25 → Mar-26 sequential sales rose (403→444→494→535) but **profit fell** (93→92→71→65). Revenue accelerating; **earnings decelerating and turning negative YoY** — the classic margin-compression signal.
**Volume/Price/Mix:** Growth has been volume + mix led (premiumisation into high-complexity). Mix is the most durable driver and is intact; however near-term mix has been *unfavourable* (lower-margin acquired entities Citieffe/PendraCare dilute consolidated margin).
**Operating leverage:** High fixed-cost manufacturing → DOL elevated; under-utilised new capacity is currently a drag (negative operating leverage). As utilisation ramps FY27–28, this reverses — the crux of the bull case.

Inflection likely FY28–29 as the current capex wave (₹296 Cr FY26 → ₹200–225 Cr FY27 guided) tapers and utilisation rises.
**Stock cycle verdict:** *Early/Mid-cycle, capex-heavy, margins suppressed, earnings decelerating.* MoS adjustment: standard-to-wider; position sizing: small until margin/utilisation inflection is *confirmed*, not just guided.
