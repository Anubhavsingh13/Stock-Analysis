## BUSINESS PRIMER

Concord Biotech makes active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) using **fermentation** — a biological manufacturing process, harder and slower than conventional chemical synthesis — where it is one of the largest global players (commanding an estimated ~20%+ global market share across several molecules such as mycophenolate, tacrolimus, sirolimus and cyclosporine). Its core franchise is **immunosuppressants** (drugs that prevent organ-transplant rejection), supplemented by growing **oncology** and **anti-infective** APIs, plus a smaller **formulations** business (finished dosage — tablets, and now injectables). Revenue is B2B and heavily export-led: it sells bulk API to generic drug makers across the US, Europe, Japan and emerging markets (exports grew 46% YoY in Q1 FY27), earning money on volume × realization of niche, hard-to-replicate molecules. Everything rests on two things: (1) sustained cost and process leadership in fermentation (deep chemistry/bioprocess IP + scale — ~1,250 m³ of fermentation capacity), and (2) the ability to keep launching new molecules (2–3 per year) as older immunosuppressants mature into cash cows.

This is a **capital-light-margin, capital-heavy-asset compounder**: a branded-quality API business with pharma-like gross economics but manufacturing-like fixed-asset intensity and unusually long working-capital cycles (fermentation batches age for months; inventory days run ~485). The key value drivers are **capacity utilization and mix** — new plants (Unit III API, Unit IV injectables) are running well below capacity (25–55%), which is *why* FY26 margins and ROCE compressed; as utilization climbs toward 80%+, operating leverage should restore 40%+ EBITDA margins. The key risks are **customer concentration and buying lumpiness** (a handful of large generic customers make quarterly and even annual revenue volatile — FY25 had a bumper Q4 that FY26 could not repeat), **regulatory dependence** (US FDA / CDSCO / EU approvals gate every launch), and a **rich valuation** that leaves no room for execution slips. A large treasury (~₹490 Cr investments) modestly flatters reported earnings via other income.
