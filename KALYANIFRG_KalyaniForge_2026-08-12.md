## Executive Summary
Kalyani Forge is a sub-scale precision-forging auto-component maker (Kalyani/Baba-Kalyani group, but a small separate listco run by Viraj Kalyani) whose revenue has been *flat for a decade* (₹241 Cr FY15 → ₹235 Cr FY26) while a genuine margin recovery (OPM 4–7% → 12–16%) and fresh OEM order wins (SKF/Schaeffler wheel hubs, xEV programs) have driven profit to a 14-year high. The thesis is an operational turnaround finally translating into growth; the key risks are that the margin jump is only 2–3 quarters old, working capital is stretched (debtor days ~160), debt has more than doubled to ₹106 Cr to fund capex, and the business remains structurally weaker and smaller than every listed peer. After an 11% single-day pop to ₹698 on Q1 FY27 results day, the margin of safety is inadequate — the turnaround is real but unproven, and the price already reflects much of it.

## BUSINESS PRIMER

Kalyani Forge makes close-tolerance precision forged, machined and sub-assembled metal components — using hot, warm and cold forging — sold almost entirely B2B to automotive OEMs and Tier-1s. Revenue is essentially **volume × realization** on engineered parts, with the mix disclosed as Engine ~60%, Driveline ~20%, Axle ~10% of sales. It operates ~5 plants (Pune region) with ~1,000 employees. The economics depend on two things: (1) forging-press and machining capacity utilisation (a fixed-cost-heavy, asset-heavy business where margin lives and dies on volume absorption), and (2) automotive production cycles at its customers. It is part of the wider Kalyani group (the same promoter family as Bharat Forge) but is a distinct, far smaller listed company — it is *not* Bharat Forge and shares none of BFL's scale, defence book, or export franchise despite the shared name.

This is a **cyclical, asset-heavy auto-ancillary** — closer to a commodity converter than a branded compounder. The key value drivers are volume (capacity utilisation) and margin (operating leverage + product mix toward higher-value machined/xEV parts); the key risks are the auto demand cycle, customer concentration, steel input-cost pass-through timing, and balance-sheet leverage funding a capacity build into an unproven demand ramp. The single structural feature that colours the entire analysis: **a decade of stagnant topline** — the business has not grown volumes in ten years, so the entire recent profit surge is a margin/mix story, not a growth story, and the investment case rests entirely on whether newly-won orders finally break that flat-revenue pattern.
