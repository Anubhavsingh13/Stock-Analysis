## Business Primer

Tanfac Industries is a fluorine-chemistry company — one of India's leading makers of **Anhydrous Hydrofluoric Acid (AHF)** and its derivatives (Aluminium Fluoride, Potassium Fluoride, sulphuric acid, oleum, and specialty fluorides). AHF is the foundational building block of the entire fluorine value chain: it feeds refrigerant gases, aluminium smelting, pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, and increasingly solar and semiconductor manufacturing. Tanfac sells largely B2B to industrial and chemical customers, and its economics are essentially a **spread business** — the margin between the realisation on its fluoride products and the cost of its key imported raw material, **fluorspar (acid-grade)**, most of which comes from China, Mexico, and Vietnam. Revenue is volume × realisation, and both the realisation (refrigerant/aluminium-fluoride prices) and the input cost (fluorspar) are cyclical commodities, so reported margins swing violently — Tanfac's operating margin has ranged from 7% to 28% over the past decade. It is a joint venture between **Anupam Rasayan India Ltd** (a specialty-fluorination major that took 24.96% and joint control from the Aditya Birla Group in March 2022) and **TIDCO** (the Tamil Nadu state industrial corporation); together they hold ~48.7%.

This is best classified as a **cyclical, commodity-linked specialty-chemical business now attempting a step-change up the value chain.** The whole investment case rests on one transformational project: a **20,000 MTPA HFC-32 refrigerant-gas plant** (₹390–405 Cr, commissioning Q3 FY27), backed by offtake MoUs covering ~65% of capacity (~₹3,673 Cr aggregate), plus long-term solar-grade DHF supply agreements (~₹1,068 Cr to FY29). The key value driver forward is the successful ramp of HFC-32 and higher-value fluorinated products at recovering margins; the key risks are three-fold and unusually specific — (1) **commodity cyclicality** (FY26 was a *down* year: PAT fell 20% to ₹70 Cr despite revenue rising 27%, as spreads compressed), (2) **execution/ramp risk** on a first-of-its-kind large plant, and (3) a **long-term regulatory sunset** — HFCs face a Kigali Amendment phase-down (freeze 2028, cuts from 2032), which caps the useful life of HFC-32 economics and is already pushing majors like SRF toward next-generation HFOs. A structural nuance colouring everything: this is a genuinely **high-return, debt-free, cash-generative** business (a rarity in commodity chemicals) — but it is being valued like a secular compounder (104× trailing earnings) while behaving like a cyclical.

## Module 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds
**Verdict: Strong secular tailwind + a cyclical margin trough — but a long-term regulatory overhang.**
- **Secular (durable):** India's refrigeration demand is projected to rise ~6.5× by 2037–38 on booming AC penetration; HFC-32 (GWP ~675, ~⅓ of R-410A) is the preferred low-GWP transition refrigerant (>40% global HVAC adoption). - **Cyclical (mean-reverts — do NOT annualise):** FY26 margins compressed (OPM 24%→16%) on weak refrigerant realisations + elevated fluorspar (India ~$560/MT). This is a *trough-ish* margin year. A cyclical recovery is plausible but must not be extrapolated as permanent.
- **Regulatory headwind (long-dated but real):** HFCs phase *down* from 2032; HFC-32's economic life is ~15–20 years, after which HFOs (where SRF is investing ₹2,300 Cr) take over. Tanfac is entering HFC-32 relatively late in its lifecycle — good for the next decade, structurally finite thereafter.
- **Raw-material/geopolitical risk:** Acid-grade fluorspar is import-dependent (China concentration) → sourcing and price risk; backward integration is a stated priority.
