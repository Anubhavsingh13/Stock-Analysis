## Executive Summary
Standard Surfactants is a ₹244 Cr-revenue (FY26) Kanpur family company that sulphonates LAB into LABSA (and, after a new Chemithon reactor and spray dryer, AOS/SLS powders) at Mandideep, and runs a low-margin IOCL polymer consignment-stockist business. For a decade it earned 2–4% operating margins and 5–11% ROCE, well below any reasonable cost of capital; Q1FY27 (Jun-26) suddenly printed a 13.9% OPM on ₹102 Cr revenue, almost certainly a **cyclical LAB/LABSA price windfall from the 2026 Strait of Hormuz supply shock** (Galaxy Surfactants' OPM also jumped to 13% the same quarter), and the stock went from ~₹52 to ₹222 in five weeks. The price now implies ~9–11% EBIT margins sustained for a decade, versus a 10-year history of ~2–4%. On top of that, governance is weak: promoter warrants were issued at ₹58 weeks before the windfall quarter, the AGM approved ₹400 Cr/yr of related-party trading limits with two family polymer-trading firms (one formed by a whole-time director who resigned the same year), there are repeated BSE LODR fines and a quorum failure, and 10-year cumulative operating cash flow is roughly zero. **AVOID.**

## Business Primer

Standard Surfactants makes and sells chemicals that let water lift grease and dirt — chiefly LABSA (linear alkyl benzene sulphonic acid), the active ingredient in most Indian washing powders, cakes and dishwash bars. It buys LAB (a crude-oil/kerosene derivative sold by an oligopoly of Reliance, IOCL, Nirma and Tamilnadu Petroproducts), reacts it with sulphur trioxide in a sulphonation reactor at its Mandideep (MP) plant, and sells the acid to detergent manufacturers, mostly small and regional B2B buyers; FY26 notes say no customer exceeds 10% of revenue and all sales are domestic (FY26 AR, Note 42). Revenue is volume × realisation, and realisation tracks LAB cost, so the business really earns a thin *conversion spread*. Recent capex added a Chemithon reactor that management says doubled sulphonation capacity and an 850 kg/hr spray dryer for AOS/SLS powders (company website). The second segment, "Others" (₹81.6 Cr, 33% of FY26 revenue), is IOCL consignment stocking/distribution of HDPE, LLDPE, PP and paraffin wax, which earns a commission-like margin of about 2.5% (FY26 AR, Note 42). The two things the business depends on are a stable LAB-to-LABSA spread and access to cheap working-capital credit, because the balance sheet is mostly receivables and inventory funded by bank lines.

This is a **commodity chemical converter plus a distribution business**: it has no brand, little pricing power, and no real technology edge. The value driver is margin (spread), and the main risk is the commodity cycle and input-price whipsaw, made worse by high leverage (D/E ≈ 2.2×) and a family-controlled governance setup with dense related-party links. Three features colour the whole analysis. (1) The latest quarter's earnings are inflated by a geopolitical LAB/LABSA price shock and should not be extrapolated. (2) A web of Garg-family entities (Icon Polymers, Icon Plastics, Standard Sulphonators, Icon Cars, etc.) trade the same polymers that SSL distributes, and shareholders have just approved up to ₹400 Cr/yr of transactions with two of them. (3) The stock is a thinly traded BSE-only microcap that has just quadrupled, so liquidity risk is extreme.

## MODULE 1b — Sector Cycle: Tailwinds & Headwinds
**Secular vs. cyclical decomposition of SSL's growth (TTM revenue +57%, Q1FY27 +93% YoY):**
- **Secular:** India surfactant demand grows ~7–9.5% a year (India surfactants market USD 2.03–2.11 bn in 2024–25; CAGR 6.8–9.5%, IMARC / MarkNtel). Use about 8% as the durable baseline.
- **Step-change volume (one-off):** the new Chemithon reactor (management says it doubled sulphonation capacity) and spray-dryer expansion were capitalised in FY25–26 (fixed assets ₹6 Cr → ₹45 Cr, Screener). This is a one-time capacity step of perhaps 15–25 pp of growth during the ramp, not a recurring rate. *Estimate; no tonnage data disclosed.*
- **Cyclical / price:** LAB and LABSA prices surged in 2026. Indian LABSA averaged USD 1.52/kg in Q1-2026 and LAB reached USD 1,988/MT in June 2026. The Hormuz blockade removed Middle-East LABSA imports, and Sasol declared force majeure (ChemAnalyst; Procurement Resource). SSL's own AR describes 10–20% chemical cost inflation and "short-term gains" for inventory holders (FY26 AR MD&A). **The rest of the growth, and most of the Q1FY27 margin jump, is cyclical and should fade within 2–4 quarters once supply normalises.**

**Input cost cycle:** Rising sharply (crude-linked LAB). In the current quarter, inventory holders gain, but margins compress once replacement costs catch up or prices fall back. **Inventory cycle:** Likely restocking / panic buying by detergent makers during the supply shock, which inflates orders above true demand. **Competitive capacity:** LABSA sulphonation is cheap to build and highly fragmented; high spreads attract the many small sulphonators to restart idle capacity (no hard data; scuttlebutt gap). **Geopolitics:** raw-material sourcing risk is HIGH (LAB is crude/kerosene-linked; Hormuz carries 50–55% of India's crude/LNG per FY26 AR). Export risk nil (100% domestic). Logistics risk: freight-route disruption. *Classification: transient for the margin windfall (it will mean-revert) but structural for cost volatility.* **Net FX exposure:** no exports; FX risk comes through imported/crude-linked LAB pricing and a foreign-currency demand loan (₹17.87 Cr at FY25, nil at FY26; AR Note 19). Not quantifiable; data gap.

**Sector cycle verdict: Tailwind *now* (cyclical, windfall) → Headwind risk as it unwinds.** Do NOT embed Q1FY27 margins in the DCF. MoS widened +10 pp for cyclical-peak earnings.
