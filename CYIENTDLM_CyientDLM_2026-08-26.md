## Executive Summary
Cyient DLM is the electronics-manufacturing-services (EMS) subsidiary of Cyient (parent owns ~52%), specialising in high-mix, low-to-medium-volume, high-reliability electronics for **aerospace & defence (42%)** and industrial (32%) customers — a sector riding a powerful India-defence-indigenisation + China+1 tailwind. Operationally the momentum is real: Q1 FY27 revenue +34% YoY, PAT +118%, a **record order book of ₹2,599 Cr (book-to-bill ~1.5×)**, and margins inching up. But the stock has run **+86% in a year to near its 52-wk high (₹798)**, trades at **~77× P/E and ~6.3× book**, and — critically — has a **serious working-capital / cash-conversion problem** (working-capital days blew out from −32 to 169; inventory 314 days; FCF negative in FY24–FY25). The business quality and tailwind are genuine; the price and the cash-flow profile are not. This is a "buy the dip, not the top" name — the current valuation prices in years of flawless execution while the balance sheet quietly absorbs the cost of growth.

## Business Primer

Cyient DLM is a "design-led" EMS provider — it doesn't sell its own branded products; it builds electronic assemblies, printed-circuit-board assemblies (PCBAs), box-builds and integrated systems to customers' designs (and increasingly co-designs them), specialising in **complex, high-reliability, low-volume** electronics where quality and traceability matter more than unit cost. Its end-markets are aerospace & defence (radar, avionics, mission systems), industrial (automation, energy, robotics, data-centre), and medical. Revenue flows through long-cycle programs backed by a firm **order book (₹2,599 Cr)** — it manufactures against customer purchase orders, so revenue is program-driven and lumpy, with material (electronic components) a large pass-through cost. As a spun-out subsidiary of Cyient, it leverages the parent's aerospace/defence customer relationships and engineering depth. The two things the business depends on are (a) a steady flow of defence/aerospace program awards, and (b) the ability to source scarce electronic components and *finance the inventory* those long programs require.

This is an **asset-medium, working-capital-heavy manufacturing business in a structural growth sector** — the value drivers are order-book conversion (volume) and gradual margin expansion (mix toward higher-value box-builds/systems and design content), and the key risks are **thin margins (~10%), heavy and deteriorating working capital, component-supply shocks, and customer/program concentration**. The single structural feature that must colour the whole analysis: EMS is a *low-margin, cash-hungry* model, and Cyient DLM's growth is being part-funded by a ballooning working-capital cycle — so reported profit growth (+118%) meaningfully overstates the economic cash return. At ~77× earnings, the market is treating a thin-margin, cash-consuming EMS as if it were a high-return compounder; that gap is the crux of the risk.
